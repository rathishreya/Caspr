## Positioning

**Tagline:** Caspr means Business.

**Double meaning — both active at all times:**
- Serious, professional, not for casual use
- Built specifically for business tasks

**Category:** Analytical AI.

**One-line positioning:**
> Caspr is Analytical AI — purpose-built for business analysis, not conversation.

**The message stack (locked 2026-08-19):**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Category** | Analytical AI |
| **Identity** | **Not an assistant. An analyst.** |
| **Promise** | **Arrive certain.** |
| **Proof** | **Every source, credible. Every claim, triangulated. Every report, defensible.** |

**Three rules that come with it:**

1. **Never name a competitor or the LLM category in lead copy.** Naming them concedes we are in the same
   category. The contrast with generative AI is *category education* — it belongs on `/vs/*` pages, in social
   and in founder content, never in a hero, a headline or an ad.
2. **Never frame proof as work for the reader.** "Verify", "check the working", "traceable", "audit it
   yourself" all sell the reader more labour, which contradicts the promise. The evidence is present and
   surfaced on demand — it is never an instruction. Citations answer *"when the room asks"*, not
   *"so you can confirm we are telling the truth."*
3. **Analyst, not advisor and not expert.** Advisor competes with the buyer — consultants, strategy teams and
   agency strategists *are* the advisor. Expert asks to be trusted on reputation, which Caspr does not have.
   An analyst is trusted for how the work was done, which is the only trust Caspr can earn.


**Why this matters:**
Every major AI player (OpenAI, Perplexity, Google, Anthropic) is building on Large Language Models optimised for language: generating text, summarising, chatting. Caspr is built differently — a proprietary system of specialised analytical models engineered for working with data, applying research methodology, and producing defensible strategic output. The architecture is different because the purpose is different.

This is not a marketing claim. It is a design choice made from day one.

**The category challenge** — *category education only, never a hero line (see rule 1 above):*
> "While the world was building generative AI, we built analytical AI."

## What Caspr Does

A user enters a single business prompt. Caspr interrogates it with clarifying questions, structures the research methodology, and returns a boardroom-ready 100-page PDF or editable PPTX — with data, charts, citations, and structured narrative — in under 15 minutes.

**Two proprietary systems:**
- **Source** — *every source, credible.* The 25M+ curated corpus, live feeds, and the client's own material
- **Weigh** — *every claim, triangulated.* Reconciles what sources disagree on; does not merely retrieve
- **Conclude** — *every report, defensible.* Structures a position, not a summary

**⛔ The Thinking Brain and the Learning Brain are retired, together (2026-08-27).** Full reasoning:
[`../docs/source-assess-conclude.md`](../docs/source-assess-conclude.md)

**25M+ curated, credible sources:** Documents, government databases, academic papers, news feeds. Not web-scraped noise.

**The evidence travels with the conclusion:** every insight rests on a cited, credible source, surfaced the moment it is asked for. The output is defensible in front of a board, a client, or an investor — without the reader having to go and confirm it.

**Primary use cases (in order of current frequency):**
1. Market research reports
2. Business cases (consulting-grade)
3. Business proposals and RFPs
4. Competitor landscape analysis
5. Due diligence (expanding as data coverage grows)

## Content Pillars

Every piece of content maps to at least one. Do not create content that maps to none.

### Pillar 1: An Analyst, Not an Assistant
The identity. Caspr does the analyst's work — reads the market, weighs what the sources disagree on, and reaches a conclusion.
- Proper research methodology — it **weighs** and **concludes**, it does not merely retrieve
- Real-time live data and a curated corpus — what it **sources**
- 25M+ curated, credible sources
- **Every source, credible. Every claim, triangulated. Every report, defensible.**
- Never framed as work for the reader: the evidence is present, never assigned

### Pillar 2: Analytical AI
The category. Built for analysis from the ground up.
- Analysis is not a conversation. It is scoped, methodical, and it concludes
- Purpose-built for business analysis, not for language
- **Category education only.** The contrast with the generative category belongs on `/vs/*` pages, in social and in founder content. It never appears in a hero, a headline or an ad — naming that category concedes we are in it

### Pillar 3: From Weeks to Minutes
Supporting, not leading. *(Demoted from Pillar 1 on 2026-08-20 — speed is commoditising and cannot carry the position.)*
- Full market analysis in under 15 minutes
- Boardroom-ready PDF or editable PPTX
- Refine sections and ask follow-ups in real time

### Pillar 4: A Fraction of the Cost
Institutional-quality analysis, without the institutional price tag. The third act, never the hook.
- A Study at $80 vs. $5,000–$50,000 from a boutique research agency (96–99% cheaper). Vs. McKinsey or BCG: a different order of magnitude — their minimum engagement exceeds $50,000
- No retainer, no contract, no relationship overhead
- First $100 free — judge the output before committing a dollar

### Pillar 5: Your Data is Your Data
Enterprise-grade information security, built in from day one — not bolted on.
- ISO 27001:2022 certified · GDPR compliant
- User-uploaded data is never used to train any model. Ever.
- Every analysis stays in your account. Not used to improve ours.
- The trust foundation for boardroom-sensitive work

**Usage note:** This pillar is never the lead. It surfaces at the moment of trust — when a professional is about to upload a data room, share a confidential brief, or put Caspr output in front of a client. Security answers the question they haven't asked yet. Mention it without making it the headline. Link every reference to `/security`.

## Voice: The Trusted Senior Analyst

Caspr speaks like the most credible person in the room. The one who arrives with the numbers already done, speaks in conclusions not caveats, and never wastes a word.

Not the friendly colleague who helps you out. The authoritative source you trust in front of a board.

The register is The Economist or the Financial Times — not a SaaS startup. Premium business press. Authoritative without being stiff. The wit is present but it doesn't try.

### Voice Attributes

| Attribute | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **Precise** | Every word earns its place. No padding, no qualifications that don't add meaning. |
| **Authoritative** | Speaks in conclusions. Confident without hedging. Never says "may" when it means "does." |
| **Dry wit** | Intelligence is the humour. An observation that makes the reader feel clever for getting it. Never announced, never explained. |
| **Commercially sharp** | Always anchors to business outcomes and numbers. "15 minutes" not "fast." "$80 vs. $50,000" not "cost-effective." |
| **Respectful** | Trusts the reader's intelligence completely. Never over-explains. |

### Calibration Examples — Approved

- *"15 minutes. 100 pages. Cited to source."*
- *"The $200,000 question. For $80."*
- *"Stop Googling. Start analyzing."*
- *"Analyst-grade insights. Without the analyst retainer."*
- *"Your competitors are still waiting for the research."*
- *"We trained our AI to think like an analyst, so you don't have to."*
- *"An analyst that works 24/7. First report free."*
- *"The analysis your board expects. Not the price."*
- *"While the world was building generative AI, we built analytical AI."*
- *"Generative AI writes. Analytical AI analyses."*
- *"Built for analysis, not conversation."*

### Calibration Examples — Not Approved

| What was written | Why it's wrong |
|---|---|
| "Our AI is totally crazy good!" | Exclamation points. Informal to the point of undermining credibility. |
| "Our platform leverages advanced algorithms to optimise your research workflow." | Pure jargon. No outcome. No human. |
| "Caspr.ai is excited to announce..." | Corporate voice. We don't get excited. We deliver. |
| "Not an LLM. A LAM." | Do not use LAM — the architecture claim is not yet ready to defend publicly. Use "Analytical AI" instead. |

## Writing Rules

1. **Pain first, feature never.** Start with the reader's experience. Arrive at the product through the problem.
2. **Conclusions, not descriptions.** Don't describe what Caspr does. Tell them what they get.
3. **Numbers anchor everything.** When you have a specific number, use it. When you don't, find one or remove the claim.
4. **Second person, always.** "You" not "users." "Your business case" not "business cases generated by the platform."
5. **No exclamation points.** Ever. Confidence does not require punctuation.
6. **Short sentences.** If a sentence has more than two clauses, split it.
7. **Lean into Analytical AI.** It is the sharpest single differentiator. Use it before describing any feature.
8. **Do not use "LAM."** Use "Analytical AI," **"Source. Assess. Conclude."**, or "purpose-built for analysis." The LAM claim is reserved for when the architecture is ready to be defended publicly.

### On Wit
Write precisely, and the wit emerges on its own. If you're trying to be witty, the copy will feel forced.

The test: would a Senior Partner at a consulting firm find this sharp — or roll their eyes?

## Founder Story (Background Credibility Asset)

Caspr is the hero of the brand. The founder story is a background signal — used on the About page, in PR, in investor materials, and as a proof point against the credibility objection. It does not lead marketing copy.

### The Story

**Joy Sharma**, co-founder and CEO, is a former McKinsey consultant (US and Middle East) who spent years advising major private sector and government organisations on strategy, national programme design, and business transformation.

After McKinsey, he founded **EZ** — a global B2B services company, recognised in the Middle East, that delivers outsourced business intelligence and analytical services to 100+ multinational organisations. EZ grew from bootstrapped startup to a profitable, multi-million dollar business with double-digit CAGR — fully independently run. Joy is now Chairman.

**Jayant**, co-founder and CTO, was recruited by Joy at EZ and rose to lead AI and Technology there. He left to co-found Caspr.

Together: the analyst who has spent 15 years knowing what the output should look like, and the engineer who built the system to produce it — within an organisation that has been delivering exactly this kind of analysis to multinationals for years.

### Why This Matters for the Brand

Caspr is not built by technologists who identified a market opportunity. It is built by someone who has:
- **Been the consultant** producing this analysis (McKinsey)
- **Run the service operation** delivering it at scale to multinationals (EZ)
- **Now automated it** (Caspr)

This directly answers the #1 credibility objection: *"Will AI-generated research be good enough to present to a board?"*

The answer: it was designed by someone who set that standard for 15 years.

### How to Use It

**Use in:** About page, PR and media, investor materials, LinkedIn thought leadership, testimonial framing, speaking engagements.

**Use as:** A proof point, not a protagonist. The copy is: *"Caspr was built by a former McKinsey consultant who spent 15 years producing exactly this kind of analysis — and then automated it."* One sentence. Then move to what Caspr does.

**Do not use:** As a headline, in ad copy, in product emails, or anywhere Caspr's capabilities should speak for themselves.

**EZ reference:** Name EZ in Middle East contexts and investor materials where it adds regional credibility. In global contexts, describe it as "a profitable, independently run B2B services business serving 100+ multinationals" without requiring name recognition.

## Caspr Academic — Sub-Brand Voice and Positioning

### What It Is

Caspr Academic is the student-facing expression of the Caspr brand. It is not a separate product — it is the same analytical engine at a pricing structure designed for academic users, explicitly positioned as Caspr's investment in the next generation of analysts.

The sub-brand sits under the Caspr master brand. The name is always **"Caspr Academic"** — not "student plan," not "student discount," not "student tier." The word "Academic" signals rigour and research, not budget concession.

### Positioning Angle: The Investment Frame

Caspr Academic is not charity pricing. It is not a discount. It is a deliberate commercial investment: Caspr accepts thin margin on students now in exchange for professional users later. This framing is true, brand-consistent, and makes Caspr look like a company that thinks in decades, not quarters.

Every Caspr Academic communication reinforces this. The brand is investing in the analyst. The analyst will one day invest in the brand.

**The positioning line:**
> *"Caspr Academic. Because the analyst in the boardroom tomorrow is the student working at midnight tonight."*

**Short form:**
> *"We invest in analysts before they can afford to invest in us."*

### Voice Modifications for Academic Audience

The core voice — the Trusted Senior Analyst — holds. But the register shifts slightly toward peer and mentor rather than authoritative source. Caspr Academic speaks like the most credible person in the MBA cohort who also happens to be telling you how to get an edge before anyone else does.

| Standard Caspr voice | Academic voice adjustment |
|---|---|
| Board-level authority | Peer-level credibility ("I've been in your position") |
| Conclusions, not descriptions | Still conclusions, but earned through relatable context |
| Dry wit, unexplained | Dry wit with one extra degree of warmth |
| Cost mentioned third | Cost mentioned third — but framed as investment return, not bargain |

**What does NOT change:** No exclamation points. No jargon. No "amazing" or "powerful." No over-explanation. The student ICP is intelligent and ambitious — condescension is the fastest way to lose them.

### Approved Copy — Caspr Academic

- *"Your library closed at 9pm. Your case brief doesn't care. Caspr is open."*
- *"Caspr Academic. Because the analyst in the boardroom tomorrow is the student working at midnight tonight."*
- *"We invest in analysts before they can afford to invest in us."*
- *"ChatGPT gives you confident text. Caspr gives you citations you can defend in front of a judge."*
- *"The industry primer your recruiting season demands. Without the library sprint."*
- *"$8 per Brief. $40 per Study. Because access to real research shouldn't depend on your institution's library budget."*
- *"Cited to source. Boardroom-ready. Available at midnight."*
- *"Analytical AI for the analyst you're becoming."*

### Naming Conventions

| Context | Use | Do not use |
|---|---|---|
| Product name | Caspr Academic | Caspr Student, Caspr Edu, Student Plan, Academic Plan |
| Pricing reference | Academic pricing | Student discount, student rate, reduced pricing |
| Earn programme | Research Dollars | Credits, points, rewards |
| Share mechanic | Share Card | Referral card, sharing link, promo link |
| Post-graduation | Professional account | Upgrade, paid account |
| Club programme | Caspr Academic Club | Club plan, team plan, group discount |

### What to Avoid

- Do not reveal that pricing is near-cost. The investment frame handles this — Caspr is investing, not discounting.
- Do not use "ChatGPT" as the primary comparison in above-the-fold copy. Use it to address the objection, not as the headline.
- Do not describe Caspr Academic as "affordable" — use "the rate students deserve" or the access/democratisation frame instead.
- Do not use "student discount" anywhere. It commoditises the sub-brand and invites professional users to feel they are paying a premium for something that has a cheaper version.

## In-Product Conversation — Voice Register

**Approved by Joy, 2026-08-18.** The register Caspr uses when the *product*
speaks to the user while an analysis runs — the engagement questions, the
product notes, the status narration. Sits beside the Academic variant above:
core voice holds, register shifts, and the list of what does not change is what
keeps it a register shift rather than a personality transplant.

**In one line: same voice, one notch quieter — and the notch is situational
awareness, not friendliness.** Call it **The Analyst Between Meetings.**

### Why the shift is deliberately small

- **The positioning caps the warmth.** *"Built for analysis, not conversation"*
  is approved copy. Every generative assistant is warm and eager; warmth is the
  competitor's signature. If this mode sounds like one of them, the category
  claim collapses at the moment the user is paying most attention.
- **The visual already carries the shift** — italic, hollow grey dot. When
  typography carries a distinction, the copy need not carry it again.
- **What changes is function, not personality.** This mode asks and narrates
  where the brand voice asserts.

### How each attribute survives

| Attribute | In conversation |
|---|---|
| **Precise** | Unchanged. The strictest of the five here |
| **Authoritative** | Expressed as **specificity and closure** — a named, answerable question with real options, never an open invitation |
| **Dry wit** | Allowed in an observation, **never in a request**. Wit while asking a favour is presumptuous |
| **Commercially sharp** | Unchanged. *"reading 1,240 sources"*, never *"lots of sources"* |
| **Respectful** | Sharpens into brevity — one clause of rationale, never a paragraph |

**Authority in a question is specificity.** *"Which sectors do you follow
most?"* with three named options is authoritative. *"Tell us about your areas of
interest"* is a form.

### Warmth comes from situational awareness, never tone

> *"While the analysis runs — which sectors do you follow most? It sharpens
> every report."*

No warm words, reads as considerate: it names the moment, asks one closed thing,
and gives the reason in one clause. Reuse that mechanism.

### Two permissions unique to this register

Neither belongs on the site or in a report:

1. **First person singular** — *"Three ways I'd show this."*
2. **Contractions** — *"I'd"*, *"doesn't"*, *"let's"*.

### Sentence form follows function

- **Asking → complete sentences.** *"Sectors followed?"* is a database field.
- **Narrating status → fragments are correct.** *"Sourcing · reading 1,240
  sources"* is right as it stands.

### What does NOT change

No exclamation points. No greetings or pleasantries. No apologising for asking
(*"sorry to interrupt"*, *"if you have a moment"*). No over-explaining the
value — one clause. No emoji. No *"let me know if you have any questions."* No
hedging the ask. And it **never claims what the engine will do** — this channel
speaks about the user and the product, never on Caspr's behalf about the
analysis.

### Approved Copy — In-Product Conversation

- *"While the analysis runs — which sectors do you follow most? It sharpens every report."*
- *"Three ways I'd show this — composition, trend, or share-shift. Pick one, or tell me."*
- *"Sourcing · reading 1,240 sources"*
- *"Who reads this one — you, or a committee?"*
- *"Noted. That sharpens the next one."*
- *"You have run four studies this month. Worth knowing: Intelligence goes deeper on the same question."*

### Not Approved — In-Product Conversation

| What was written | Why it's wrong |
|---|---|
| *"Hi Joy! While we work on that, mind if I ask a quick question?"* | Greeting, exclamation, apology for asking — three failures in one line |
| *"Sectors followed?"* | A form field. Curt where the brand is precise |
| *"Tell us a bit about the kind of research you do — it really helps us tailor things for you!"* | Open-ended, padded, exclamation. Reads as a survey |
| *"Great choice! I'll make sure to weight that in the analysis."* | Praise, exclamation, and a promise about the engine the product cannot make |
| *"I'm so glad you asked."* | Chatbot. The fastest way to lose the category claim |
| *"Just checking in while that runs…"* | *"Just"* hedges, and nothing is being asked |

### The two tests

Every line passes both, or it does not ship.

1. **The chatbot test** — *would this be at home in a generative assistant?* If
   yes, cut it. Defends the positioning.
2. **The consultant test** — *would a good consultant say this, to this person,
   at this moment?* If no, say nothing. Defends the user's time.

The second permits **silence**, which is a legitimate output. The first is the
one people fail, because warmth is the default instinct in product copy and here
it is the risk.

## Security Claims — Approved Language

Caspr has ISO 27001:2022 certification and GDPR compliance. The following claims are cleared for use in all marketing. Jayant has confirmed the "no model training" claim is accurate.

### Approved Short-Form (for inline use, linked to /security)

- *"ISO 27001:2022 certified."*
- *"GDPR compliant."*
- *"SOC 2 Type I audit in progress."*
- *"Your data never trains our models."*
- *"Your uploaded documents never reach an external AI provider."*
- *"AES-256 encrypted at rest. TLS 1.3 in transit."*

### Approved Long-Form (for /security page, enterprise materials, FAQ)

- *"Caspr is ISO 27001:2022 certified and GDPR compliant. Your uploaded documents are processed entirely within Caspr's own infrastructure — they are never sent to any external AI provider. Chat text that reaches Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google is governed by Zero Data Retention agreements — not stored, not used for training. AES-256 at rest, TLS 1.3 in transit. ISO 27001:2022 certification means these controls are independently audited, not self-reported. SOC 2 Type I audit is currently in progress."*

### One Claim That Needs Care

**The two-tier architecture** — the fact that Caspr's core research models are self-hosted (and the providers are Chinese AI companies) is a strong privacy claim but a potential enterprise concern if providers are named publicly. Always describe it as: *"Your uploaded documents are processed within Caspr's infrastructure."* Full provider detail is for enterprise procurement, not marketing copy.

### Integration Rules

1. **Every security reference links to `/security`.** No exceptions. This creates the trust chain: inline signal → /security full answer → /pricing conversion.
2. **Security appears at the decision moment, not the discovery moment.** Place it near CTAs for data-sensitive actions (data room upload, enterprise inquiry, account signup), not in hero sections.
3. **Never make security the headline.** The product sells on speed, quality, and cost. Security answers a concern — it does not open the door.
4. **Use the certification, not a description.** "ISO 27001:2022 certified" is more credible than "enterprise-grade security." The name of the standard is the claim.
5. **For enterprise copy:** Add one security line to every enterprise-facing asset below the primary value proposition and before the CTA.

### What Not to Say

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| "We take security seriously" | Empty. Every company says this. |
| "Enterprise-grade security" | Vague. The certification is the proof, not the label. |
| "Your data is safe" | Unverifiable claim. Say what specifically makes it safe. |
| Detailed subprocessor lists in marketing copy | Belongs on /security, not in ads or email |
