## Executive Summary

The report provides a detailed analysis of CompuGroup Medical (CGM), a prominent European health IT platform. In FY2024, CGM reported a revenue of EUR 1.154 billion, with a significant portion (74%) being recurring. The company anticipates growth in FY2025, projecting a revenue of EUR 1.213 billion, which demonstrates its strong appeal to investors seeking consistent cash flow. The report compares CGM’s performance against other global and European competitors, focusing on metrics such as revenue quality, market share, and strategic positioning, particularly in the DACH region and Western Europe, with notable competition from major U.S. vendors like Epic and Oracle Health.

The global health IT market, poised for significant growth by 2026, is characterized by a mid-teens growth rate. The report highlights AI workflow automation, cloud migration, and modernization of pharmacy and lab systems as key growth drivers. The market is expected to be worth between USD 403 billion and USD 999 billion by then, with software enhancing clinician productivity as a major investment focus.

CGM is positioned as a leading health IT vendor in Europe, with a diverse portfolio across different healthcare settings focusing on recurring revenue through strategic projects in hospital information and pharmacy systems. The report notes CGM's strategic emphasis on cloud migration and AI integration while facing competition in the U.S from vendors like Epic and athenahealth in acute care and ambulatory markets.

The competitive landscape section underscores CGM's position compared to competitors such as Epic and Oracle, highlighting its smaller scale but stronger recurring revenue visibility. CGM’s focus on modernization and strategic positioning in the DACH region is emphasized, although it faces pressure to accelerate cloud services and enhance its innovation capabilities.

The report discusses M&A activities, noting a strategic shift in the market from scaling to platform densification, driven by recurring workflows and AI adoption. CGM is positioned as an attractive player in this landscape due to its recurring revenue base and potential for strategic acquisitions that can leverage existing workflows and data.

In the SWOT analysis, CGM’s strengths lie in its strong regional presence and robust revenue model. However, challenges like cloud adoption, customer advocacy, and profitability improvement are noted. The report suggests growth opportunities through localization and AI enhancements, aiming to leverage CGM’s existing installed base and workflow advancements.

The valuation section presents CGM as a solid investment, though not a leader due to its margin constraints and market visibility challenges. Potential growth is seen through cloud and AI monetization, and the report offers strategic recommendations focusing on core market strengths, product integration, and setting clear M&A criteria to enhance valuation and competitive standing. The report concludes with a call for improved platform convergence and capital allocation discipline to strengthen CGM’s market position.

## 2. Global Health IT Market Landscape

Even with that caveat, the directional conclusion is consistent: the market is expanding at a mid-teens rate, with 2026 global estimates spanning roughly USD 403 billion to USD 999 billion and long-range growth expectations of about 16 percent CAGR, supported by provider digitization, AI-enabled workflow automation, cloud migration, interoperability mandates, and pharmacy and lab modernization.[49](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/healthcare-it-market-109359#:~:text=The%20global%20healthcare%20IT,during%20the%20forecast%20period) [50](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/healthcare-it-market#:~:text=Electronic%20Health%20Records%20%28EHRs%29,for%20patient%20history%20queries) [53](https://www.360iresearch.com/library/intelligence/healthcare-it#:~:text=The%20Global%20Healthcare%20IT,531.50%20billion%20in%202026)

For investors and M&A professionals, the more important takeaway than any single top-down number is where value is accruing. Growth is concentrating in software and data layers that improve clinician productivity, automate revenue and administrative workflows, enable cross-setting data exchange, and create AI-ready longitudinal records, while legacy on-premises estates in hospital, ambulatory, pharmacy, and laboratory environments remain the monetizable installed base that incumbents such as CGM, Dedalus, Oracle Health, Epic, NEXUS, and regional specialists are trying to migrate rather than replace outright.[58](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/digital-health.html#:~:text=See%20latest%20analysis%3A%20Towards,Information%20System%20in%20Korea) [59](https://www.who.int/health-topics/digital-health#:~:text=The%20purpose%20of%20WHO%E2%80%99s,data%20system%20in%20Nepal) [72](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/key-initiatives/burden-reduction/interoperability/policies-and-regulations#:~:text=CMS%20also%20published%20a,Authorization%20Final%20Rule%20%28CMS-0057-F%29)

### 2.1. Market Size, Growth Trajectory, and Key Trends

Reported market size varies materially by taxonomy, but the growth signal is unambiguous. Fortune Business Insights values the global healthcare IT market at USD 354.0 billion in 2025 and USD 402.7 billion in 2026, implying a 2026 to 2034 CAGR of 16.65 percent, while Grand View Research places the market at USD 866.4 billion in 2025 and USD 998.8 billion in 2026 with a 16.2 percent CAGR through 2033; 360iResearch estimates USD 531.5 billion for 2026 after USD 464.4 billion in 2025.[49](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/healthcare-it-market-109359#:~:text=The%20global%20healthcare%20IT,during%20the%20forecast%20period) [50](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/healthcare-it-market#:~:text=Electronic%20Health%20Records%20%28EHRs%29,for%20patient%20history%20queries) [53](https://www.360iresearch.com/library/intelligence/healthcare-it#:~:text=The%20Global%20Healthcare%20IT,531.50%20billion%20in%202026)

- Interpretation for investors: The spread in absolute market size reflects scope differences, not disagreement on direction. Across methodologies, health IT remains one of the faster-growing healthcare software categories into 2026, which supports premium valuation frameworks for vendors with recurring revenue, migration pathways, and AI monetization optionality.[49](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/healthcare-it-market-109359#:~:text=The%20global%20healthcare%20IT,during%20the%20forecast%20period) [50](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/healthcare-it-market#:~:text=Electronic%20Health%20Records%20%28EHRs%29,for%20patient%20history%20queries)
- Demand drivers: Growth is being pulled by provider labor shortages, documentation burden, remote and ambulatory care expansion, payer and provider interoperability requirements, and the need to modernize fragmented core systems rather than continue funding bespoke interfaces and manual workflows.[58](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/digital-health.html#:~:text=See%20latest%20analysis%3A%20Towards,Information%20System%20in%20Korea) [59](https://www.who.int/health-topics/digital-health#:~:text=The%20purpose%20of%20WHO%E2%80%99s,data%20system%20in%20Nepal) [78](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/cms-interoperability-prior-authorization-final-rule-cms-0057-f#:~:text=Including%20Denial%20Reason%3A%20Beginning,the%20prior%20authorization%20request)
- AI trend: AI is shifting from point pilots to embedded workflow tooling in documentation, summarization, coding, inbox management, and decision support. An August 2026 arXiv paper notes that an estimated 70 to 80 percent of healthcare AI pilots fail to scale because of governance, data fragmentation, and integration gaps, which is strategically favorable for incumbent platform vendors that control workflow, identity, and data access layers.[41](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06112#:~:text=Despite%20explosive%20growth%20of,Management%20System%20%28HIMS%29%20flows)
- Cloud trend: Cloud adoption is no longer a discretionary architecture choice. It is increasingly the prerequisite for continuous deployment, API-based interoperability, AI model orchestration, and lower total cost of ownership, especially in ambulatory and mid-market hospital settings.[50](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/healthcare-it-market#:~:text=Electronic%20Health%20Records%20%28EHRs%29,for%20patient%20history%20queries) [58](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/digital-health.html#:~:text=See%20latest%20analysis%3A%20Towards,Information%20System%20in%20Korea)
- Capital markets implication: The market is rewarding vendors that can convert maintenance-heavy installed bases into subscription, platform, and transaction revenue. In practical terms, this favors companies with strong migration economics and penalizes vendors whose legacy estates require high service intensity without clear cloud or AI upsell paths.[59](https://www.who.int/health-topics/digital-health#:~:text=The%20purpose%20of%20WHO%E2%80%99s,data%20system%20in%20Nepal) [58](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/digital-health.html#:~:text=See%20latest%20analysis%3A%20Towards,Information%20System%20in%20Korea)

### 2.3. Macro Forces Shaping Competitive Dynamics

The competitive landscape in 2026 is being shaped less by feature parity in core records and more by who can operationalize compliant data liquidity. AI, cloud, interoperability, and regulation are now interdependent rather than separate themes, and vendors that cannot align all four are at risk of margin compression, slower bookings, or installed base attrition.[58](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/digital-health.html#:~:text=See%20latest%20analysis%3A%20Towards,Information%20System%20in%20Korea) [59](https://www.who.int/health-topics/digital-health#:~:text=The%20purpose%20of%20WHO%E2%80%99s,data%20system%20in%20Nepal) [41](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06112#:~:text=Despite%20explosive%20growth%20of,Management%20System%20%28HIMS%29%20flows)

- AI as a platform test: Generative AI is moving procurement criteria toward workflow orchestration, data quality, and governance. The practical implication is that incumbents with embedded clinical workflows and broad data access can defend share, but only if they can productize AI safely and at scale rather than rely on isolated copilots.[41](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06112#:~:text=Despite%20explosive%20growth%20of,Management%20System%20%28HIMS%29%20flows) [44](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00073#:~:text=Date%3A%20Tue%20Aug%2026,data%20landscape%20in%20healthcare)
- Cloud economics: Cloud migration is reshaping competitive dynamics by lowering deployment friction, enabling continuous upgrades, and supporting API-first architectures. It also changes revenue recognition and valuation quality by increasing subscription and managed service mix, which is strategically relevant for investors comparing legacy license vendors with cloud-native or cloud-transitioning peers.[50](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/healthcare-it-market#:~:text=Electronic%20Health%20Records%20%28EHRs%29,for%20patient%20history%20queries) [58](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/digital-health.html#:~:text=See%20latest%20analysis%3A%20Towards,Information%20System%20in%20Korea)
- Interoperability pressure in the United States: U.S. regulation continues to push vendors toward standardized data exchange and lower information friction. ONC’s HTI rule set updates certification and information sharing requirements, while CMS states that under the Interoperability and Prior Authorization Final Rule, impacted payers must provide specific reasons for denied prior authorization decisions beginning in 2026, increasing the value of integrated payer-provider workflow software.[74](https://healthit.gov/regulations/hti-rules#:~:text=This%20naming%20convention%20started,the%20first%20HTI-1%20rule) [78](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/cms-interoperability-prior-authorization-final-rule-cms-0057-f#:~:text=Including%20Denial%20Reason%3A%20Beginning,the%20prior%20authorization%20request)
- Interoperability pressure in Europe: The European Health Data Space Regulation was published on March 5, 2025 and entered into force on March 26, 2025, with phased implementation beginning later in the decade. Even before full application, EHDS is already influencing procurement by favoring vendors that can support standardized exchange, patient access, and secondary use governance across member states.[69](https://health.ec.europa.eu/ehealth-digital-health-and-care/european-health-data-space-regulation-ehds_en#:~:text=the%20EHDS%20will%20enable,researchers%2C%20regulators%2C%20and%20innovators) [70](https://health.ec.europa.eu/latest-updates/regulation-eu-2025327-european-health-data-space-and-amending-directive-201124eu-and-regulation-eu-2025-03-05_en#:~:text=%23%20Regulation%20%28EU%29%202025%2F327,Food%20Safety%20Public%20Health)
- AI regulation in Europe: The European Commission states that the EU AI Act entered into force on August 1, 2024, with phased applicability thereafter. For health IT vendors, this raises compliance costs but also creates a barrier to entry that may advantage scaled incumbents able to fund documentation, risk management, and post-market controls across clinical AI use cases.[71](https://health.ec.europa.eu/ehealth-digital-health-and-care/artificial-intelligence-healthcare_en#:~:text=the%20European%20Artificial%20Intelligence,Act%29%20entered%20into%20force) [82](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-113#:~:text=This%20Regulation%20shall%20enter,from%202%20August%202027)
- Macro and budget backdrop: OECD analysis indicates health systems remain under pressure to improve efficiency, workforce productivity, and data governance while managing constrained budgets and reform agendas. That environment supports ROI-led purchasing, bolt-on automation, and consolidation around fewer strategic platforms, which is favorable for vendors with recurring revenue and cross-setting breadth but challenging for subscale point solution providers.[62](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/03/latest-health-spending-trends-and-outlook_2db269d5/9fc8d4b1-en.pdf#:~:text=initiatives%20to%20strengthen%20%2Ahealth,enhance%20health%20system%20efficiency) [58](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/digital-health.html#:~:text=See%20latest%20analysis%3A%20Towards,Information%20System%20in%20Korea)

### 3.2. Geographic Footprint, Installed Base, and Recurring Revenue Profile

CGM’s geographic profile is unusually broad for a European listed health IT vendor, but economically concentrated in Germany and Western Europe. The company had offices in 19 countries and distributed products in 60 countries as of the 2024 and 2025 reporting periods, giving it a diversified route to market while preserving a home market bias that supports regulatory fit, local workflow depth, and customer retention.[102](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/0d2df5326c5fc1baaa5f85eb2cbdacc62df07b6a/250306_2024_CGM%20Annual%20Report.pdf) [100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=financial%20year%202025%20was,solution%20for%20medical%20practices)

- Regional revenue concentration: In 2025, Germany contributed EUR 684.5 million of third-party revenue, or about 56 percent of group sales, while Western Europe contributed EUR 356.1 million, North America EUR 133.4 million, Eastern Europe EUR 33.5 million, and the rest of the world EUR 5.5 million. This mix confirms that CGM is fundamentally a DACH and Western European platform with a smaller but still relevant U.S. business, rather than a balanced global vendor.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=financial%20year%202025%20was,solution%20for%20medical%20practices)
- Installed base logic: CGM’s customer backbone spans physicians, dentists, hospitals, pharmacies, laboratories, insurers, and pharmaceutical companies, which creates a cross-setting installed base that is broader than a pure EHR vendor and more locally embedded than most U.S. acute care peers. For investors, that breadth matters because it supports cross-sell into connectivity, patient communication, document automation, and data services without requiring greenfield customer acquisition in every workflow.[102](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/0d2df5326c5fc1baaa5f85eb2cbdacc62df07b6a/250306_2024_CGM%20Annual%20Report.pdf)
- Recurring revenue resilience: Group recurring revenue was EUR 853.9 million in 2024 and EUR 880.5 million in 2025, representing 74 percent and about 73 percent of revenue respectively. The durability of this base is reinforced by maintenance contracts, software leases, SaaS, and recurring service models that are embedded in daily provider and pharmacy operations.[102](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/0d2df5326c5fc1baaa5f85eb2cbdacc62df07b6a/250306_2024_CGM%20Annual%20Report.pdf) [100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=financial%20year%202025%20was,solution%20for%20medical%20practices)
- Segment-level recurrence: In 2025, recurring revenue shares were 66 percent in AIS, 70 percent in HIS, and 65 percent in PCS. The notable point is that even the hospital segment, despite its project orientation and public tender exposure, now carries a majority recurring profile, which improves visibility relative to traditional perpetual license hospital software models.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=financial%20year%202025%20was,solution%20for%20medical%20practices)
- Market-specific stickiness: Germany remains the strongest retention anchor because CGM is deeply exposed to national infrastructure and workflow layers such as e-prescription processing, telematics, and ambulatory practice software. The company noted that more than 90,000 medical facilities in Germany issue e-prescriptions and over 17,000 pharmacies process them, a market structure that favors incumbents with embedded connectivity and compliance tooling.[102](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/0d2df5326c5fc1baaa5f85eb2cbdacc62df07b6a/250306_2024_CGM%20Annual%20Report.pdf)
- Portfolio implication: Compared with U.S. mega vendors that dominate acute care but have narrower exposure to pharmacy and country-specific ambulatory workflows in Europe, CGM’s installed base is less concentrated by customer type and more diversified by care setting. That diversification supports downside resilience, but it also increases product and go-to-market complexity, which can weigh on execution and valuation if modernization is uneven.[102](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/0d2df5326c5fc1baaa5f85eb2cbdacc62df07b6a/250306_2024_CGM%20Annual%20Report.pdf) [100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=financial%20year%202025%20was,solution%20for%20medical%20practices)

### 3.3. Strategic Priorities and Innovation Pipeline

CGM’s current strategy is less about expanding into entirely new categories and more about upgrading the monetization and defensibility of its installed base. Through 2025 and into 2026, management has emphasized cloud migration, AI-embedded workflow tools, open platform architecture, and operational excellence, with the stated 2026 outlook calling for low- to mid-single-digit organic revenue growth and a slight increase in cash EBITDA margin.[92](https://www.cgm.com/corp_en/magazine/articles/press-releases/2026-1/compugroup-medical-maximizes-customer-benefit-with-targeted-product-innovations.html#:~:text=CompuGroup%20Medical%20expects%20organic,the%20cash%20EBITDA%20margin) [100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=financial%20year%202025%20was,solution%20for%20medical%20practices)

- AI as product layer: CGM states it has been strategically investing in artificial intelligence, machine learning, and large language models since 2023, with the effort intensified in 2025. Existing examples include the CGM ONE telephone assistant, DokuAssistant, CGM HIS Assistant, and the U.S. ambient AI product CGM AMBI, indicating that AI is being deployed first in documentation, communication, and administrative workflow compression rather than high-risk clinical decisioning.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=financial%20year%202025%20was,solution%20for%20medical%20practices) [102](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/0d2df5326c5fc1baaa5f85eb2cbdacc62df07b6a/250306_2024_CGM%20Annual%20Report.pdf) [93](https://www.cgm.com/usa_en/articles/dossiers/press-releases.html#:~:text=Lab%20Software%20%7C%20Daniel,experience%20in%20the%20industryLeading)
- Cloud migration in AIS: The flagship ambulatory modernization program is CGM Praxis on the cloud-based CGM ONE platform, which management describes as a new cloud AIS and end-to-end solution for medical practices. Add-ons such as TelefonAssistent, Self CheckIn, AmbulApps, and DokuAssistent show a deliberate move from classic practice software toward modular automation services that can raise ARPU and improve retention.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=financial%20year%202025%20was,solution%20for%20medical%20practices) [92](https://www.cgm.com/corp_en/magazine/articles/press-releases/2026-1/compugroup-medical-maximizes-customer-benefit-with-targeted-product-innovations.html#:~:text=CompuGroup%20Medical%20expects%20organic,the%20cash%20EBITDA%20margin)
- Hospital platform strategy: In HIS, CGM is expanding the CGM CLINICAL Suite on open CGM G3 technology and positioning CGM CLINICAL RCM as a successor path around the SAP IS-H transition. This is strategically important because it gives CGM a time-sensitive opening to capture modernization budgets in German-speaking hospital markets where legacy administrative and billing architectures are being reconsidered.[92](https://www.cgm.com/corp_en/magazine/articles/press-releases/2026-1/compugroup-medical-maximizes-customer-benefit-with-targeted-product-innovations.html#:~:text=CompuGroup%20Medical%20expects%20organic,the%20cash%20EBITDA%20margin) [102](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/0d2df5326c5fc1baaa5f85eb2cbdacc62df07b6a/250306_2024_CGM%20Annual%20Report.pdf)
- Pharmacy digitization: In PCS, CGM is pushing cloud-based pharmacy software through CGM STELLA, first highlighted in Italy and reiterated in 2026 product communications. The investment case here is that pharmacy remains a high-retention vertical where cloud delivery can reduce support burden, improve update cadence, and create a platform for adjacent data and workflow services.[102](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/0d2df5326c5fc1baaa5f85eb2cbdacc62df07b6a/250306_2024_CGM%20Annual%20Report.pdf) [92](https://www.cgm.com/corp_en/magazine/articles/press-releases/2026-1/compugroup-medical-maximizes-customer-benefit-with-targeted-product-innovations.html#:~:text=CompuGroup%20Medical%20expects%20organic,the%20cash%20EBITDA%20margin)
- Security and compliance as enablers: CGM highlighted C5 attestation for its cloud services in Germany in the 2024 annual report, framing security assurance as a prerequisite for healthcare cloud adoption. In European provider IT, this is not a marketing detail but a commercial gating factor, especially for AI-enabled and hosted workflows in regulated ambulatory and hospital settings.[102](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/0d2df5326c5fc1baaa5f85eb2cbdacc62df07b6a/250306_2024_CGM%20Annual%20Report.pdf)
- Innovation spend and execution burden: CGM employed an average of 2,759 R&D staff in 2025 and reported EUR 274 million of R&D expense, up from EUR 255 million in 2024. That level of spend is strategically necessary to modernize a broad portfolio, but for investors it also means the key question is not whether CGM is investing enough, but whether it can convert that spend into faster cloud migration, better segment margins, and a clearer platform narrative versus more focused competitors.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=financial%20year%202025%20was,solution%20for%20medical%20practices)
- Strategic read-through: CGM’s innovation pipeline is credible because it is tied to existing workflows and installed customers rather than speculative moonshots. The competitive edge will depend on speed of rollout and commercial packaging, since rivals such as Epic, Oracle Health, Dedalus, and NEXUS are also repositioning around cloud, interoperability, and AI, often with simpler product architectures or stronger acute care concentration.

### 4.2. Oracle Health (Cerner): Market Position, Customer Attrition, and AI Platform Pivot

Oracle Health remains one of the few vendors with enough installed base, capital, and technical ambition to matter strategically despite deteriorating customer sentiment. KLAS reported that Oracle Health was one of only five vendors with validated net-new acute care decisions in 2025, but separate KLAS analysis and secondary coverage indicate the company has lost meaningful market share and customer confidence in the three years since Oracle acquired Cerner.[29](https://engage.klasresearch.com/blog/uncertainty-on-multiple-fronts-slowed-ehr-purchases-insights-from-klass-2026-acute-care-ehr-market-share-report/10709/#:~:text=KLAS%20validated%20net-new%20EHR,Oracle%20Health%2C%20and%20TruBridge) [113](https://klasresearch.com/report/oracle-health-2025/3827#:~:text=It%20has%20been%20three,2025%20KLAS%20Research%2C%20LLC) [115](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ehrs/oracle-health-loses-ground-3-years-after-cerner-acquisition-klas/#:~:text=Oracle%20Health%20has%20struggled,between%202022%20and%202025) [116](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/oracle-health-lost-customers-satisfaction-stagnated-cerner-acquisition-klas-research/758427/#:~:text=Oracle%20also%20acquired%20a,delivered%20on%20some%20promises)

Oracle’s current position is therefore paradoxical. It still controls 21.9 percent of U.S. acute care hospitals and 20.4 percent of beds according to 2025 KLAS-based reporting, yet that scale now functions more as a restructuring platform than as evidence of competitive momentum.[117](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ehrs/hospital-ehr-market-share-leaders-in-2025-by-category/#:~:text=While%20Epic%20was%20the,to%20the%20IT%20researcher) [121](https://medcloudinsider.com/articles/2026/07/29/oracle-health-cloud-native-ehr-rebuild.aspx#:~:text=KLAS%27s%20%22US%20Acute%20Care,hospitals%20the%20year%20before)

- Attrition and satisfaction: KLAS’s 2025 Oracle Health report examined customer interviews from 2022 through 2025 and found that Oracle had not materially improved customer experience post-acquisition. Secondary summaries describe declining relationships, communication issues, and dissatisfaction with execution, which are classic precursors to replacement cycles in high-switching-cost software markets.[113](https://klasresearch.com/report/oracle-health-2025/3827#:~:text=It%20has%20been%20three,2025%20KLAS%20Research%2C%20LLC) [115](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ehrs/oracle-health-loses-ground-3-years-after-cerner-acquisition-klas/#:~:text=Oracle%20Health%20has%20struggled,between%202022%20and%202025) [116](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/oracle-health-lost-customers-satisfaction-stagnated-cerner-acquisition-klas-research/758427/#:~:text=Oracle%20also%20acquired%20a,delivered%20on%20some%20promises)
- Market position: Oracle Health remains the number two acute care EHR vendor by U.S. hospital share, which means it still has strategic relevance in every large replacement discussion even when it is not the favored bidder.[117](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ehrs/hospital-ehr-market-share-leaders-in-2025-by-category/#:~:text=While%20Epic%20was%20the,to%20the%20IT%20researcher) [29](https://engage.klasresearch.com/blog/uncertainty-on-multiple-fronts-slowed-ehr-purchases-insights-from-klass-2026-acute-care-ehr-market-share-report/10709/#:~:text=KLAS%20validated%20net-new%20EHR,Oracle%20Health%2C%20and%20TruBridge)
- Platform pivot: Oracle is attempting to reset the narrative through a cloud-native rebuild and AI-led product positioning rather than incremental modernization of Cerner Millennium. Industry reporting in 2026 indicates Oracle plans to expand its new OCI-based EHR architecture into acute care, making 2026 to 2027 a proving period for whether technical reinvention can offset installed-base erosion.[121](https://medcloudinsider.com/articles/2026/07/29/oracle-health-cloud-native-ehr-rebuild.aspx#:~:text=KLAS%27s%20%22US%20Acute%20Care,hospitals%20the%20year%20before) [114](https://engage.klasresearch.com/blog/klas-reports-published-in-august-2025/8386/#:~:text=The%20report%20is%20also,about%20Oracle%E2%80%99s%20road%20map)
- AI strategy: Oracle Health has emphasized Clinical AI Agent and broader AI-enabled workflow tooling. The strategic logic is sound because Oracle can combine infrastructure, database, and application layers, but customer willingness to adopt depends on restoring trust in core delivery first.[114](https://engage.klasresearch.com/blog/klas-reports-published-in-august-2025/8386/#:~:text=The%20report%20is%20also,about%20Oracle%E2%80%99s%20road%20map)
- Organizational risk: Bloomberg reported in March 2026 that Oracle’s health unit had lost multiple senior leaders while trying to modernize the platform, which increases execution risk during an already delicate transition.[123](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-02/oracle-loses-health-unit-leaders-with-ellison-s-bet-struggling#:~:text=Oracle%20Corp.%E2%80%99s%20health%20records,of%20steep%20customer%20loses)
- Read-through for CGM: Oracle Health is a weaker near-term execution benchmark than Epic, but it remains a credible long-term threat because Oracle can subsidize product redevelopment and pursue aggressive AI and cloud bundling. For CGM, Oracle’s instability creates tactical openings in hospital replacement cycles, especially in Europe, but those openings may narrow if Oracle’s platform reset gains traction after 2026.[113](https://klasresearch.com/report/oracle-health-2025/3827#:~:text=It%20has%20been%20three,2025%20KLAS%20Research%2C%20LLC) [100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf)

### 4.3. Athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, and Meditech: Ambulatory and Niche Positioning

Below the Epic and Oracle tier, athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, and MEDITECH occupy durable positions that matter to CGM because they define the economics of ambulatory, community, and mid-market provider IT. None matches Epic’s acute care dominance, but each controls a segment where workflow fit, customer retention, and recurring revenue quality can be highly attractive.[156](https://www.athenahealth.com/press-releases/athenahealth-secures-five-2026-best-in-klas-awards#:~:text=We%E2%80%99re%20also%20proud%20to,and%20patient%20engagement%20capabilities) [135](https://www.eclinicalworks.com/about-us/#:~:text=and%20consistently%20profitable%2C%20with,revenues%20of%20%24900%20million) [158](https://ehr.meditech.com/global/meditech-south-africa/awards#:~:text=MEDITECH%20secures%20Best%20in,Accounting%3A%20small%20%281-150%20beds%29)

athenahealth is the strongest ambulatory benchmark in this group. The company won five 2026 Best in KLAS awards, including Overall Independent Physician Practice Suite for the third consecutive year, and is positioning athenaOne as an AI-native, network-enabled operating layer for independent and enterprise ambulatory groups.[156](https://www.athenahealth.com/press-releases/athenahealth-secures-five-2026-best-in-klas-awards#:~:text=We%E2%80%99re%20also%20proud%20to,and%20patient%20engagement%20capabilities) [139](https://www.athenahealth.com/about/who-we-are#:~:text=M0286) [175](https://engage.klasresearch.com/press-release/epic-athenahealth-chartis-optimum-healthcare-it-and-impact-advisors-win-2026-overall-best-in-klas-awards/)

- Athenahealth: Its strategic strength is the combination of cloud delivery, embedded RCM services, and a large connected network, which makes it more than a software vendor and supports premium retention in physician practices. June 2026 company disclosures highlighted more than 80 AI-native revenue cycle features, underscoring that athenahealth is using automation to defend both software ARPU and service economics.[139](https://www.athenahealth.com/about/who-we-are#:~:text=M0286) [146](https://www.athenahealth.com/about/press-releases#:~:text=2026%20athenahealth%20Introduces%20Mobile,focus%20on%20patient%20care) [156](https://www.athenahealth.com/press-releases/athenahealth-secures-five-2026-best-in-klas-awards#:~:text=We%E2%80%99re%20also%20proud%20to,and%20patient%20engagement%20capabilities)
- eClinicalWorks: eClinicalWorks remains a scaled private ambulatory vendor with 2024 revenue of USD 900 million and 6,500 employees according to its company overview. Its positioning is strongest in cost-conscious ambulatory, multispecialty, and community settings where breadth, integrated PM and RCM, and large installed-base familiarity matter more than premium enterprise branding.[135](https://www.eclinicalworks.com/about-us/#:~:text=and%20consistently%20profitable%2C%20with,revenues%20of%20%24900%20million) [138](https://www.eclinicalworks.com/new/#:~:text=PRISMA%20exchanges%202.6%20million%20patient%20records%20every%20day)

- MEDITECH: MEDITECH remains the most defensible non-Epic acute care niche vendor in the U.S. community and mid-market hospital segment. ### 5.2. Revenue, EBITDA Margins, and Recurring Revenue Ratios

- CGM baseline: CGM reported FY2025 revenue of EUR 1.213 billion, adjusted EBITDA of EUR 240 million, and an adjusted EBITDA margin of 16%, with recurring revenue of EUR 880.5 million or about 73% of sales. For investors, that places CGM below the largest U.S. and European acute care platforms on scale, but above many regional peers on disclosed revenue visibility and recurrence.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf)
- NEXUS comparison: NEXUS generated EUR 261.5 million of 2024 revenue and EUR 57.6 million of EBITDA, implying an EBITDA margin of about 22.0%. Its software maintenance revenue was EUR 140.6 million, equal to roughly 53.8% of sales, which is lower than CGM’s recurring mix but still indicative of a sticky maintenance-led model.[211](https://www.nexus-ag.de/unternehmen/investor-relations/finanzberichte?cid=115861&file=files%2Fnexus-ag%2FUnternehmen%2FInvestor+Relations%2FFinanzberichte%2F2024%2FNEXUS+Annual+Report+2024.pdf#:~:text=Nexus%20AG%20%2F%20Annual,the%20Consolidated%20Financial%20Statements) [216](https://www.nexus-ehealth.com/company/news/article/very-successful-financial-year-2024#:~:text=EBITDA%20increased%20by%2014.4%25,Report%202024.pdf%2013.7%20MiB)
- Epic scale: Forbes reported Epic had USD 4.9 billion of annual revenue in 2024, up from about USD 4.6 billion in 2022. Epic does not publicly disclose EBITDA or recurring revenue ratios, but its private ownership, large installed base, and broad suite economics strongly imply superior cash generation and reinvestment capacity relative to CGM.[239](https://www.forbes.com/sites/katiejennings/2024/09/30/epic-systems-billionaire-judy-faulkner-private-independent-never-ipo/#:~:text=With%20no%20plans%20to,and%20employee-owned%20in%20perpetuity) [144](https://www.forbes.com/sites/katiejennings/2024/03/18/why-46-billion-health-records-giant-epic-is-betting-big-on-generative-ai/)
- Oracle scale and margin context: Oracle reported total fiscal 2026 revenue of USD 57.4 billion and continues to absorb Oracle Health within broader cloud and applications reporting, so a clean Oracle Health EBITDA benchmark is not publicly disclosed. For benchmarking purposes, Oracle Health benefits from parent-level capital and infrastructure scale, but segment opacity reduces comparability for standalone M&A-style analysis.[232](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1341439/0001193125-26-277521-index.html#:~:text=Mailing%20Address%202300%20ORACLE,SIC%3A%207372%20Services-Prepackaged%20Software) [235](https://d1f19qmytqk9eo.cloudfront.net/edgar0105/2026/06/22/1341439/000119312526277521/document/orcl-20260531.htm#:~:text=A%20discussion%20regarding%20our,Relations%20website%20at%20www.oracle.com%2Finvestor)
- eClinicalWorks benchmark: eClinicalWorks states that it was consistently profitable and generated USD 900 million of revenue in 2024. The absence of audited public EBITDA disclosure limits precision, but the combination of cloud delivery, ambulatory concentration, and profitability suggests a margin profile that is likely structurally healthier than hospital-heavy legacy vendors, though not directly comparable to CGM’s multi-segment mix.[135](https://www.eclinicalworks.com/about-us/#:~:text=And%20it%E2%80%99s%20all%20eClinicalWorks,%E2%80%93%20in%20the%20cloud)
- Dedalus benchmark: Dedalus remains financially opaque as a private sponsor-backed company, but S&P Global Ratings cited about EUR 771 million of pro forma 2021 revenue and EUR 116 million of S&P-adjusted EBITDA, while Ardian said the DXC healthcare software acquisition would bring the combined business to around EUR 700 million of turnover with about 2,000 R&D staff. More recent operating commentary from Dedalus DACH points to about EUR 460 million of 2025 revenue in DACH alone, implying that group scale remains at least comparable to, and likely above, CGM in hospital-centric Europe.[234](https://www.spglobal.com/ratings/en/regulatory/article/-/view/sourceId/12530619#:~:text=The%20company%20is%20about,and%20high%20customer%20retention) [229](https://www.ardian.com/news-insights/press-releases/ardian-portfolio-company-dedalus-acquires-dxc-technologys-healthcare#:~:text=Managing%20Director%20Ardian%20Buyout%2C,heights%20it%20has%20reached) [230](https://www.kma-online.de/aktuelles/it-digital-health/detail/kis-markt-2026-trends-anbieter-und-strategien-im-ueberblick-55364#:~:text=Immer%20wieder%20geisterten%20in,seine%20Marktposition%20weiter%20ausbauen)
- Revenue quality conclusion: CGM’s 73% recurring revenue ratio is one of the strongest disclosed metrics in the peer set and is a meaningful valuation support. The trade-off is that its 16% adjusted EBITDA margin in 2025 sits below NEXUS’s 2024 EBITDA margin and below what investors would typically infer for highly scaled private leaders such as Epic, reflecting CGM’s broader portfolio complexity and ongoing transformation costs.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf) [211](https://www.nexus-ag.de/unternehmen/investor-relations/finanzberichte?cid=115861&file=files%2Fnexus-ag%2FUnternehmen%2FInvestor+Relations%2FFinanzberichte%2F2024%2FNEXUS+Annual+Report+2024.pdf#:~:text=Nexus%20AG%20%2F%20Annual,the%20Consolidated%20Financial%20Statements) [239](https://www.forbes.com/sites/katiejennings/2024/09/30/epic-systems-billionaire-judy-faulkner-private-independent-never-ipo/#:~:text=With%20no%20plans%20to,and%20employee-owned%20in%20perpetuity)

### 5.3. R&D Investment Intensity and Innovation Spend as a Proportion of Revenue

- CGM intensity: CGM reported EUR 274 million of R&D expense in 2025 on EUR 1.213 billion of revenue, equal to roughly 22.6% of sales, up from EUR 255 million in 2024. That is a high reinvestment rate for a mature health IT vendor and signals that management is funding a broad modernization agenda across ambulatory, hospital, pharmacy, cloud, and AI layers simultaneously.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf) [17](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/0d2df5326c5fc1baaa5f85eb2cbdacc62df07b6a/250306_2024_CGM_Annual%20Report.pdf#:~:text=Annual%20Report%202024CompuGroup%20Medical%20SE%20%26%20Co)
- NEXUS intensity: NEXUS does not present R&D in the same headline format as CGM, but its 2024 annual report shows capitalized development costs and a software-heavy operating model, while total investments in intangible assets and property, plant and equipment were EUR 10.4 million. On disclosed data, NEXUS appears materially less R&D-intensive than CGM, which is consistent with its narrower scope and more focused hospital software footprint.[211](https://www.nexus-ag.de/unternehmen/investor-relations/finanzberichte?cid=115861&file=files%2Fnexus-ag%2FUnternehmen%2FInvestor+Relations%2FFinanzberichte%2F2024%2FNEXUS+Annual+Report+2024.pdf#:~:text=Nexus%20AG%20%2F%20Annual,the%20Consolidated%20Financial%20Statements)

Even though current group R&D expense is not publicly disclosed, that staffing profile indicates innovation spend at a scale that likely exceeds CGM in hospital-centric software, especially when measured by absolute headcount.[229](https://www.ardian.com/news-insights/press-releases/ardian-portfolio-company-dedalus-acquires-dxc-technologys-healthcare#:~:text=Managing%20Director%20Ardian%20Buyout%2C,heights%20it%20has%20reached)
- Epic and Oracle: Neither Epic nor Oracle Health discloses a clean health-IT-only R&D ratio. Oracle’s consolidated R&D is substantial at the parent level, but it is not attributable specifically to Oracle Health, while Epic’s private status prevents precise benchmarking despite clear evidence of heavy product reinvestment in AI, interoperability, and acute care workflows.[232](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1341439/0001193125-26-277521-index.html#:~:text=Mailing%20Address%202300%20ORACLE,SIC%3A%207372%20Services-Prepackaged%20Software) [239](https://www.forbes.com/sites/katiejennings/2024/09/30/epic-systems-billionaire-judy-faulkner-private-independent-never-ipo/#:~:text=With%20no%20plans%20to,and%20employee-owned%20in%20perpetuity)
- eClinicalWorks and athenahealth: Publicly reliable, current R&D ratios were not located for either company in this search. That absence itself is analytically relevant, because it means CGM offers investors more transparency on innovation intensity than several important private peers, even if those peers may be investing aggressively in AI and cloud productization.[135](https://www.eclinicalworks.com/about-us/#:~:text=And%20it%E2%80%99s%20all%20eClinicalWorks,%E2%80%93%20in%20the%20cloud) [156](https://www.athenahealth.com/press-releases/athenahealth-secures-five-2026-best-in-klas-awards)
- Investor conclusion: CGM’s R&D burden is both a strength and a valuation constraint. It supports the thesis that the company is actively rebuilding its platform economics, but at more than one-fifth of revenue it also highlights how much capital is required to modernize a broad legacy estate, which helps explain why CGM’s margin profile trails more focused or more scaled peers despite strong recurring revenue quality.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf) [229](https://www.ardian.com/news-insights/press-releases/ardian-portfolio-company-dedalus-acquires-dxc-technologys-healthcare#:~:text=Managing%20Director%20Ardian%20Buyout%2C,heights%20it%20has%20reached) [211](https://www.nexus-ag.de/unternehmen/investor-relations/finanzberichte?cid=115861&file=files%2Fnexus-ag%2FUnternehmen%2FInvestor+Relations%2FFinanzberichte%2F2024%2FNEXUS+Annual+Report+2024.pdf#:~:text=Nexus%20AG%20%2F%20Annual,the%20Consolidated%20Financial%20Statements)

## 6. Product and Capability Benchmarking

This section benchmarks CGM’s product stack against major U.S. and European health IT vendors on the dimensions that increasingly determine strategic value creation in 2026: breadth of core EHR and HIS functionality, cloud delivery maturity, interoperability architecture, and the ability to operationalize AI and analytics inside daily workflows. The investor conclusion is that CGM remains credible and increasingly modern in ambulatory, pharmacy, and selected hospital workflows, but it still trails the top U.S. platforms and the strongest hospital-centric peers in platform unification, cloud operating maturity, and scaled AI commercialization.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf) [175](https://engage.klasresearch.com/press-release/epic-athenahealth-chartis-optimum-healthcare-it-and-impact-advisors-win-2026-overall-best-in-klas-awards/) [282](https://ehr.meditech.com/ehr-solutions/meditech-expanse#:~:text=an%20innovative%20leader%20in,clinics%2C%20and%20rehabilitation%20services)

The competitive pattern is bifurcated. Epic, athenahealth, and increasingly MEDITECH present more coherent product narratives around integrated workflows and continuous delivery, while Oracle Health is attempting a high-risk but potentially powerful reset around OCI and Clinical AI Agent; European rivals such as Dedalus and NEXUS remain relevant because local workflow depth and regulatory fit still matter in DACH and Western Europe, even when their public product disclosure is less granular than U.S. peers.[263](https://www.athenahealth.com/solutions/athenaone#:~:text=Streamlined%20workflows%20and%20expert,work%20for%20your%20staff) [255](https://www.oracle.com/health/clinical-suite/clinical-ai-agent/#:~:text=Learn%20how%20Beacon%20Health,operations%20through%20intelligent%20automation) [211](https://www.nexus-ag.de/unternehmen/investor-relations/finanzberichte?cid=115861&file=files%2Fnexus-ag%2FUnternehmen%2FInvestor+Relations%2FFinanzberichte%2F2024%2FNEXUS+Annual+Report+2024.pdf)

## 7. Regional Competitive Analysis

Regional competitive dynamics are central to CGM’s investment case because the company’s economic moat is geographically uneven. In DACH and Western Europe, CGM benefits from regulatory localization, ambulatory and pharmacy workflow entrenchment, and a broad installed base, while in the United States it competes from a narrower ambulatory and specialty position against vendors with materially greater scale, KLAS visibility, and enterprise referencability such as Epic, Oracle Health, athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, and MEDITECH.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf) [185](https://klasresearch.com/report/global-non-us-ehr-market-share-2024-large-regional-decisions-impacted-a-broad-number-of-hospitals/3325#:~:text=ezCaretech%20%28BESTCare%201.0%20to,to%20Java%2FHTML5%20code%20base%29) [26](https://klasresearch.com/report/2026-best-in-klas-awards-software-and-services/3906#:~:text=The%20Best%20in%20KLAS,professionals%20improve%20patient%20care)

Its disclosed 2025 DACH revenue of about EUR 460 million and Orbis footprint of 850 German facilities and 900 DACH facilities indicate greater acute-care density than CGM, which gives Dedalus stronger referencability in large hospital tenders and modernization cycles.[230](https://www.kma-online.de/aktuelles/it-digital-health/detail/kis-markt-2026-trends-anbieter-und-strategien-im-ueberblick-55364#:~:text=Nicht%20nur%20der%20Umsatz,Einrichtungen%20mit%20dem%20KIS)
- Global encroachment: Epic is not yet a mass-market DACH ambulatory competitor, but it is increasingly relevant in Western European flagship hospital procurements. KLAS reported Epic as the largest global EHR vendor among measured non-US vendors, while Dedalus added the most beds outside the U.S., underscoring that CGM faces pressure from both a premium global suite vendor and a scaled European hospital consolidator.[185](https://klasresearch.com/report/global-non-us-ehr-market-share-2024-large-regional-decisions-impacted-a-broad-number-of-hospitals/3325#:~:text=ezCaretech%20%28BESTCare%201.0%20to,to%20Java%2FHTML5%20code%20base%29) [184](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ehrs/epic-is-no-1-worldwide-after-adding-356-hospitals-last-year/#:~:text=Dedalus%20is%20gaining%20ground,all%20migrations%20are%20complete)
- Secondary regional challengers: NEXUS remains strategically relevant because it combines a smaller revenue base with solid profitability and sticky hospital workflows. Its 2024 revenue of EUR 261.5 million and EBITDA of EUR 57.6 million imply a focused competitor that can defend specialty and hospital-adjacent niches even if it lacks CGM’s cross-setting breadth.[216](https://www.nexus-ehealth.com/company/news/article/very-successful-financial-year-2024#:~:text=EBITDA%20increased%20by%2014.4%25,Report%202024.pdf%2013.7%20MiB)
- Investor read-through: In DACH and Western Europe, CGM is best viewed as a defensible regional platform rather than the outright hospital share leader. The valuation upside comes from converting local density into higher-ARPU cloud, AI, and workflow services before Dedalus deepens hospital control and before Epic captures more premium cross-border reference sites.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf) [185](https://klasresearch.com/report/global-non-us-ehr-market-share-2024-large-regional-decisions-impacted-a-broad-number-of-hospitals/3325#:~:text=ezCaretech%20%28BESTCare%201.0%20to,to%20Java%2FHTML5%20code%20base%29)

### 7.2. United States: CGM's Niche Positioning vs. Epic, Oracle, and Athenahealth Dominance

CGM’s U.S. position is strategically relevant but clearly niche relative to the dominant platforms. North America contributed EUR 133.4 million of CGM’s FY2025 revenue, which is meaningful as a diversification vector but small compared with the scale and market power of Epic, Oracle Health, athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, and MEDITECH in their core U.S. segments.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf)

The competitive challenge is structural. Epic remains the top overall health system suite in the 2026 Best in KLAS awards, athenahealth repeated as the top overall independent physician practice suite for the third consecutive year, and eClinicalWorks continues to operate at substantial ambulatory scale with stated 2024 revenue of USD 900 million, leaving limited whitespace for a subscale entrant without a sharply differentiated workflow proposition.[157](https://engage.klasresearch.com/press-release/epic-athenahealth-chartis-optimum-healthcare-it-and-impact-advisors-win-2026-overall-best-in-klasawards/#:~:text=Overall%20Independent%20Physician%20Practice,the%20third%20consecutive%20year) [156](https://www.athenahealth.com/press-releases/athenahealth-secures-five-2026-best-in-klas-awards#:~:text=%23%23%23%23%23%20PRESS%20RELEASE%20February,Physician%20Practice%20Suite%20Win) [135](https://www.eclinicalworks.com/about-us/#:~:text=and%20consistently%20profitable%2C%20with,revenues%20of%20%24900%20million)

- Market structure: U.S. acute care remains highly concentrated around Epic, Oracle Health, and MEDITECH, while ambulatory mindshare is led by Epic and athenahealth in the independent suite segment. This concentration raises customer acquisition costs and makes broad-based share capture unrealistic for CGM without acquisition or a highly differentiated specialty strategy.[26](https://klasresearch.com/report/2026-best-in-klas-awards-software-and-services/3906#:~:text=The%20Best%20in%20KLAS,professionals%20improve%20patient%20care) [314](https://klasresearch.com/report/comprehensive-ambulatory-care-ehr-and-pm-suites-2026-how-ambulatory-care-organizations-leverage-vendor-suites-and-third-parties-to-achieve-outcomes/3895#:~:text=Epic%20%26%20athenahealth%20Lead,2026%20KLAS%20Research%2C%20LLC)
- CGM’s likely lane: CGM’s U.S. opportunity is more plausibly in ambulatory, specialty, lab-adjacent, and AI-enabled documentation workflows than in enterprise acute care displacement. The company’s U.S. product messaging around CGM AMBI and adjacent ambulatory solutions suggests a strategy of workflow augmentation rather than direct confrontation with Epic or Oracle in large hospital core systems.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf)
- Competitive benchmark: athenahealth is the most relevant U.S. comparator for CGM’s ambulatory ambitions because it combines cloud delivery, RCM integration, and strong KLAS recognition. Its June 3, 2026 disclosure of more than 80 AI-native revenue cycle features shows how far the leading ambulatory platforms have moved beyond core EHR into automation-led operating leverage.[146](https://www.athenahealth.com/about/press-releases#:~:text=Physician%20sentiment%20survey%20provides,burnout%20and%20other%20challenges) [156](https://www.athenahealth.com/press-releases/athenahealth-secures-five-2026-best-in-klas-awards#:~:text=%23%23%23%23%23%20PRESS%20RELEASE%20February,Physician%20Practice%20Suite%20Win)
- Regulatory burden: U.S. interoperability requirements continue to raise the bar for all vendors. ONC states that HTI-1 adopts USCDI Version 3 as the new baseline standard in the certification program as of January 1, 2026, which favors vendors with larger compliance budgets and mature API ecosystems.[311](https://healthit.gov/regulations/hti-rules/hti-1-final-rule/#:~:text=Adopts%20the%20United%20States,of%20January%201%2C%202026)
- Investor read-through: CGM’s U.S. business should be valued as an option on focused ambulatory and AI workflow growth, not as a credible near-term challenger to the dominant acute-care stack. The strategic merit lies in selective niches that can scale without requiring head-on competition against Epic’s enterprise dominance, Oracle’s installed base, or athenahealth’s ambulatory brand strength.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf) [157](https://engage.klasresearch.com/press-release/epic-athenahealth-chartis-optimum-healthcare-it-and-impact-advisors-win-2026-overall-best-in-klasawards/#:~:text=Overall%20Independent%20Physician%20Practice,the%20third%20consecutive%20year)

### 7.3. Emerging Markets and International Expansion Trajectories

CGM’s international expansion logic is more selective than that of the largest global EHR vendors. The company reported offices in 19 countries and product distribution in 60 countries, but FY2025 revenue outside Germany, Western Europe, North America, and Eastern Europe remained very small, with only EUR 5.5 million reported in the rest-of-world category, indicating that international optionality exists but is not yet a material earnings driver.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf) [17](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/0d2df5326c5fc1baaa5f85eb2cbdacc62df07b6a/250306_2024_CGM_Annual%20Report.pdf#:~:text=Annual%20Report%202024CompuGroup%20Medical%20SE%20%26%20Co)

The opportunity set is nevertheless real because many non-U.S. markets remain less consolidated than the American acute-care market and still reward localized deployment models. KLAS’s global non-U.S. market-share work shows a broad field of regional decisions and migrations across Europe, the Middle East, Asia-Pacific, and Latin America, which implies that sub-global vendors can still win where procurement is fragmented and local adaptation matters.[185](https://klasresearch.com/report/global-non-us-ehr-market-share-2024-large-regional-decisions-impacted-a-broad-number-of-hospitals/3325#:~:text=ezCaretech%20%28BESTCare%201.0%20to,to%20Java%2FHTML5%20code%20base%29)

- Most plausible expansion vector: Eastern Europe and selected adjacent international markets are more realistic for CGM than greenfield entry into highly consolidated Anglo markets. CGM already generated EUR 33.5 million of FY2025 revenue in Eastern Europe, providing a modest but existing platform from which to extend ambulatory, hospital, and pharmacy offerings.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf)
- Why emerging markets matter: In less mature provider IT markets, buyers often prioritize localization, implementation flexibility, and total cost of ownership over the prestige of a global mega-suite. That dynamic can favor CGM’s modular portfolio, especially where ambulatory and pharmacy digitization are earlier in the adoption curve and where hospital replacement cycles are not yet locked into Epic-style enterprise standards.[185](https://klasresearch.com/report/global-non-us-ehr-market-share-2024-large-regional-decisions-impacted-a-broad-number-of-hospitals/3325#:~:text=ezCaretech%20%28BESTCare%201.0%20to,to%20Java%2FHTML5%20code%20base%29)
- Constraint set: The same factors that create opportunity also create execution risk. Emerging markets often require local partnerships, country-specific compliance work, and lower price points, which can dilute margins unless CGM exports standardized cloud products rather than replicating its historically fragmented on-premises model.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf)
- Competitive context: Dedalus and Epic are also expanding internationally, with KLAS identifying Dedalus as the vendor adding the most beds outside the U.S. in the 2024 cycle and Epic as the largest global EHR vendor among measured vendors. This means CGM’s international path is unlikely to be scale-led and must instead rely on targeted country selection, ambulatory and pharmacy adjacency, and M&A or partnership-led entry.[185](https://klasresearch.com/report/global-non-us-ehr-market-share-2024-large-regional-decisions-impacted-a-broad-number-of-hospitals/3325#:~:text=ezCaretech%20%28BESTCare%201.0%20to,to%20Java%2FHTML5%20code%20base%29) [293](https://www.dedalus.com/global/en/media/news/dedalus-identified-as-ehr-vendor-with-most-contracted-beds-outside-the-us-for-3-consecutive-years-by-klas-research/#:~:text=According%20to%20the%20newly,in%20the%20past%20five)
- Investor read-through: Emerging markets should be viewed as medium-term option value rather than a near-term rerating catalyst. The more credible upside case is that CGM uses selective international expansion to add incremental growth and strategic scarcity value, while the core valuation still depends on defending and monetizing DACH and Western Europe.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf) [185](https://klasresearch.com/report/global-non-us-ehr-market-share-2024-large-regional-decisions-impacted-a-broad-number-of-hospitals/3325#:~:text=ezCaretech%20%28BESTCare%201.0%20to,to%20Java%2FHTML5%20code%20base%29)

## 8. Customer Satisfaction and Market Perception

Customer satisfaction is a material valuation variable in provider IT because replacement cycles are infrequent, implementations are disruptive, and referenceability strongly influences future bookings. Across the peer set, the 2025 to mid-2026 evidence shows a clear hierarchy: Epic and athenahealth continue to lead U.S. perception benchmarks, MEDITECH remains unusually resilient in its community and mid-market niche, Dedalus is the strongest independently visible hospital satisfaction benchmark in DACH and Southern Europe, and Oracle Health remains the clearest negative outlier on customer confidence. CGM sits in a more mixed position, with credible satisfaction signals in selected ambulatory revenue cycle workflows and a management narrative explicitly centered on rebuilding trust, but with materially less third-party benchmark visibility than the largest U.S. and European leaders.[26](https://klasresearch.com/report/2026-best-in-klas-awards-software-and-services/3906#:~:text=Epic%20EpicCare%20Inpatient%20EHR%2FResolute%20Hospital%20Billing) [157](https://engage.klasresearch.com/press-release/epic-athenahealth-chartis-optimum-healthcare-it-and-impact-advisors-win-2026-overall-best-in-klasawards/#:~:text=TeleVox%20customers%20report%20a,offerings%20in%20healthcare%20IT) [14](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/compugroup-medical-annual-report-260305-cgm-annual-report-2025-ce7e5fdad088f226#:~:text=The%20general%20partner%20submitted,to%20the%20Supervisory%20Board) [29](https://engage.klasresearch.com/blog/uncertainty-on-multiple-fronts-slowed-ehr-purchases-insights-from-klass-2026-acute-care-ehr-market-share-report/10709/#:~:text=KLAS%20validated%20net-new%20EHR,Oracle%20Health%2C%20and%20TruBridge)

The most plausible consolidation scenarios for CGM over the next several years fall into three buckets: continued bolt-on acquisitions in Europe, selective portfolio reshaping under private ownership, or a larger strategic transaction if a buyer seeks immediate scale in DACH and Western European provider IT. - Pharmacy cloud migration: PCS combines high retention with superior margins, and cloud products such as CGM STELLA create a path to lower support intensity and higher ARPU. This is one of the clearest near-term hospital openings available to regional incumbents.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=CompuGroup%20Medical%20expects%20the,revenues%20in%20total%20revenues)
- Selective Eastern Europe and adjacent markets: CGM already generated EUR 33.5 million of FY2025 revenue in Eastern Europe, providing a foothold for expansion into less consolidated provider IT markets where local adaptation and total cost of ownership remain decisive.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=CompuGroup%20Medical%20expects%20the,revenues%20in%20total%20revenues)
- AI-enabled installed-base monetization: CGM does not need to outspend Epic or Oracle to create value. It can use ambient documentation, summarization, call automation, and workflow copilots to raise retention and wallet share inside existing accounts, especially in ambulatory and pharmacy settings.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=CompuGroup%20Medical%20expects%20the,revenues%20in%20total%20revenues) [251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11402#:~:text=Date%3A%20Tue%20Aug%2011%2020%3A07%3A41%202026)

- M&A whitespace: Fragmented European ambulatory, pharmacy, and compliance software markets still offer tuck-in targets that can be migrated onto CGM’s platform. ### 10.4. Threats from Global Platform Players, New Entrants, and Disruptive Models

The external threat environment is intensifying because the market is consolidating around fewer strategic platforms while AI lowers the perceived differentiation of legacy front-end workflows. CGM is therefore exposed both to large incumbents with superior capital and referenceability, and to modular challengers that can attack high-value workflow layers without replacing the full core system.[343](https://www.dedalus.com/anz/media/news/dedalus-ranked-1-in-2026-best-in-klas-software-services-for-acute-care-ehr-in-dach-and-southern-europe/#:~:text=Dedalus%20has%20once%20again,ORBIS%20and%20Dedalus%20Care) [250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14618#:~:text=Title%3A%20FHIRconnect%3A%20Towards%20a,of%20standardized%20transformation%20mechanisms)

- Epic and premium enterprise encroachment: Epic remains the benchmark for large-system acute care execution and customer trust. Even where it is not a direct ambulatory or pharmacy competitor, its presence in flagship hospital tenders can reset buyer expectations around integration, AI, and implementation quality.[343](https://www.dedalus.com/anz/media/news/dedalus-ranked-1-in-2026-best-in-klas-software-services-for-acute-care-ehr-in-dach-and-southern-europe/#:~:text=Dedalus%20has%20once%20again,ORBIS%20and%20Dedalus%20Care)
- Dedalus as the most immediate European threat: Dedalus’s repeated number one KLAS position in DACH acute care EHR and its hospital-centric scale make it the clearest structural threat to CGM’s hospital ambitions in Western Europe. It is the competitor most likely to compress CGM’s pricing power and referenceability in regional hospital procurements.[343](https://www.dedalus.com/anz/media/news/dedalus-ranked-1-in-2026-best-in-klas-software-services-for-acute-care-ehr-in-dach-and-southern-europe/#:~:text=Dedalus%20has%20once%20again,ORBIS%20and%20Dedalus%20Care)
- Cloud-native ambulatory challengers: athenahealth and eClinicalWorks demonstrate how integrated cloud EHR, PM, RCM, and patient engagement stacks can outcompete more heterogeneous architectures on speed, usability, and release cadence. Their model raises the risk that CGM’s ambulatory base could face margin pressure if cloud migration is too slow.[386](https://klasresearch.com/best-in-klas-ranking/ambulatory-rcm-services-ehr-associated/2026/60#:~:text=2026%20Best%20In%20Klas%23,performance%20score%20%28100-point%20scale%29) [100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=CompuGroup%20Medical%20expects%20the,revenues%20in%20total%20revenues)
- Oracle optionality risk: Oracle Health is currently weaker on customer sentiment than Epic, but Oracle’s capital base and OCI-led rebuild mean it cannot be dismissed. If the platform reset succeeds after 2026, Oracle could re-enter European and international hospital cycles with aggressive bundling and AI-led positioning.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=CompuGroup%20Medical%20expects%20the,revenues%20in%20total%20revenues)
- Interoperability-driven modular disruption: As FHIR tooling, transformation engines, and API ecosystems mature, customers may buy best-of-breed workflow layers without committing to a single incumbent suite. That can erode the historical moat of installed-base vendors if they do not own the most valuable automation and data-exchange layers.[250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14618#:~:text=Title%3A%20FHIRconnect%3A%20Towards%20a,of%20standardized%20transformation%20mechanisms) [253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05822#:~:text=including%20expanded%20FHIR%20profile,clinical%20validation%2C%20are%20discussed)
- AI abstraction risk: Ambient documentation, summarization, and patient communication are increasingly being delivered by specialized vendors and infrastructure partners. If these layers become vendor-agnostic, some of CGM’s planned AI differentiation could be competed away into lower-margin feature parity.[251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11402#:~:text=Date%3A%20Tue%20Aug%2011%2020%3A07%3A41%202026)
- Regulatory cost inflation: European and U.S. interoperability, security, and AI governance requirements raise barriers to entry, but they also increase compliance cost for incumbents with broad legacy estates. Larger global players may absorb those costs more efficiently than CGM can.[5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.08695#:~:text=97%25%20of%20approved%20AI%2FML-enabled,this%20space%20%5BFDA%2C%202024%5D) [100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=CompuGroup%20Medical%20expects%20the,revenues%20in%20total%20revenues)

The investor takeaway is that CGM’s threat profile is less about sudden displacement and more about gradual strategic compression. If management fails to accelerate platform unification, cloud conversion, and customer proof points, the company risks remaining a durable but lower-multiple regional incumbent while better-capitalized or more coherent rivals capture the premium layers of future value creation.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=CompuGroup%20Medical%20expects%20the,revenues%20in%20total%20revenues) [216](https://www.nexus-ehealth.com/company/news/article/very-successful-financial-year-2024#:~:text=EBITDA%20increased%20by%2014.4%25,Report%202024.pdf%2013.7%20MiB) [343](https://www.dedalus.com/anz/media/news/dedalus-ranked-1-in-2026-best-in-klas-software-services-for-acute-care-ehr-in-dach-and-southern-europe/#:~:text=Dedalus%20has%20once%20again,ORBIS%20and%20Dedalus%20Care)

## 11. Valuation and Investment Considerations

Relative to listed software and health IT comparables, the company benefits from a high recurring revenue base and defensible DACH positioning, but its multiple support is constrained by lower margins than best-in-class software peers, a still-elevated transformation burden, and limited public benchmark visibility versus premium assets such as Epic or athenahealth, which remain private and therefore unavailable for direct public-market multiple comparison.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=The%20guidance%20was%20prepared,in%20financial%20year%202026%3A) [431](https://www.marketscreener.com/quote/stock/COMPUGROUP-MEDICAL-SE-CO--191076936/#:~:text=Profile%20Governance%20Share%20ownership%20Insider%20transactions%20Group%20Connections) [211](https://www.nexus-ag.de/unternehmen/investor-relations/finanzberichte?cid=115861&file=files%2Fnexus-ag%2FUnternehmen%2FInvestor+Relations%2FFinanzberichte%2F2024%2FNEXUS+Annual+Report+2024.pdf)

### 11.2. Key Financial Risks and Return Drivers for Investors and M&A Professionals

CGM’s 2025 annual report shows revenue of EUR 1.213 billion, adjusted EBITDA of EUR 240 million, net income of EUR 29 million, and net debt of EUR 720.1 million, which leaves limited room for prolonged margin underperformance if R&D intensity, implementation costs, or restructuring drag remain elevated.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=The%20guidance%20was%20prepared,in%20financial%20year%202026%3A) [21](https://ca.marketscreener.com/news/compugroup-medical-annual-report-260305-cgm-annual-report-2025-ce7e5fdad088f226#:~:text=%7C%20772%2C815%20%7C%20-,%7C%20-%207%20%25)

### 11.3. Strategic Scenarios: Standalone Growth, Partnership, or Acquisition

The most credible strategic scenarios for CGM are not symmetrical. Standalone execution remains the base case, partnership is the most capital-efficient accelerator, and a full acquisition is the highest-upside but lower-probability path unless valuation remains modest relative to strategic scarcity.

- Standalone growth case: This is the highest-probability scenario. ## 12. Strategic Outlook and Recommendations

CGM’s post-2026 strategic outlook is best framed as a contest between installed-base monetization and strategic compression. The company’s FY2025 revenue of EUR 1.213 billion, recurring revenue of EUR 880.5 million, and management guidance for low to mid single-digit organic growth with slight cash EBITDA margin improvement indicate a stabilization and rebuilding phase rather than a breakout share-capture cycle.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=Advanced%20HCIT%20solutions%20with,and%20diverse%20data%20sets) [444](https://www.cgm.com/corp_en/data/downloads/presse/260224-pr-financial-year-2025-en.html#:~:text=The%202025%20annual%20report,on%20March%205%2C%202026)

### 12.3. Strategic Recommendations for Positioning, Differentiation, and Value Creation

CGM’s strategic agenda should prioritize fewer, more monetizable control points rather than broad portfolio ambition. The company already has enough installed-base breadth to matter strategically, but the next phase of value creation depends on simplifying the story for customers, investors, and potential acquirers.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=Advanced%20HCIT%20solutions%20with,and%20diverse%20data%20sets) [364](https://www.nexus-ehealth.com/company/news/article/european-e-health-technology-group-nexus-ag-and-scandinavian-ai-specialist-omilon-join-forces)

- Prioritize ambulatory and pharmacy as the primary value-creation core: Management should explicitly allocate capital and go-to-market focus toward AIS and PCS, where recurring revenue, retention, and margin structure are strongest. For investors, this would improve mix quality and reduce dependence on lower-margin hospital project work.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=Advanced%20HCIT%20solutions%20with,and%20diverse%20data%20sets)
- Reframe hospital strategy around selective control points, not full-spectrum leadership: CGM should compete where it has a timing or localization edge, especially hospital RCM, administrative modernization, and SAP IS-H successor pathways, while avoiding capital-intensive attempts to match Epic or Dedalus across every acute-care workflow. This would improve win rates and reduce implementation risk.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=Advanced%20HCIT%20solutions%20with,and%20diverse%20data%20sets) [344](https://www.dedalus.com/global/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2026/02/Dedalus-Press-release-Best-in-KLAS-2026-VF-0502206-1.pdf#:~:text=Dedalus%20has%20once%20again,ORBIS%20and%20Dedalus%20Care)
- Productize interoperability as a margin lever: CGM should turn integration capability into standardized middleware, mapping libraries, and deployment accelerators rather than continuing to absorb it as bespoke services work. The technical direction supported by recent arXiv literature suggests that reusable transformation frameworks can lower delivery cost, improve stickiness, and create a differentiated European data-liquidity layer.[250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14618#:~:text=Title%3A%20FHIRconnect%3A%20Towards%20a,of%20standardized%20transformation%20mechanisms) [253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05822#:~:text=Date%3A%20Fri%20Jan%209,data%20across%20healthcare%20systems)
- Use AI to deepen retention and ARPU, not to chase headline parity: CGM should focus AI investment on ambient documentation, call automation, summarization, coding support, and patient communication inside existing workflows. This is more realistic than trying to outbuild Oracle or out-reference Epic, and it aligns with the broader evidence that scalable healthcare AI depends on workflow integration and data governance rather than model novelty alone.[251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11402#:~:text=Date%3A%20Tue%20Aug%2011%2020%3A07%3A41%202026) [100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=Advanced%20HCIT%20solutions%20with,and%20diverse%20data%20sets)
- Tighten M&A criteria: Future acquisitions should be limited to tuck-ins that accelerate cloud migration, AI documentation, pharmacy adjacency, or country-specific compliance depth. CGM’s own history and the broader market, including NEXUS’s Omilon acquisition, suggest that capability densification is more value-accretive than large-scale horizontal expansion at this stage.[364](https://www.nexus-ehealth.com/company/news/article/european-e-health-technology-group-nexus-ag-and-scandinavian-ai-specialist-omilon-join-forces) [100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=Advanced%20HCIT%20solutions%20with,and%20diverse%20data%20sets)
- Create explicit investor milestones for rerating: Management should report progress against a small set of externally legible KPIs, including cloud migration penetration, attach rates for AI and workflow modules, recurring revenue durability above 70 percent, and margin improvement by segment. Without clearer proof points, the market is likely to continue valuing CGM as a durable but lower-multiple incumbent rather than a strategic scarcity asset.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf#:~:text=Advanced%20HCIT%20solutions%20with,and%20diverse%20data%20sets)
- Strategic end-state recommendation: CGM should position itself as the leading sovereign workflow and interoperability platform for ambulatory, pharmacy, and selected hospital-adjacent processes in DACH and Western Europe. That positioning is narrower than a global suite ambition, but it is more defensible, more margin-accretive, and more likely to support both public-market rerating and strategic optionality in future M&A scenarios.[444](https://www.cgm.com/corp_en/data/downloads/presse/260224-pr-financial-year-2025-en.html#:~:text=The%202025%20annual%20report,on%20March%205%2C%202026) [344](https://www.dedalus.com/global/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2026/02/Dedalus-Press-release-Best-in-KLAS-2026-VF-0502206-1.pdf#:~:text=Dedalus%20has%20once%20again,ORBIS%20and%20Dedalus%20Care) [29](https://engage.klasresearch.com/blog/uncertainty-on-multiple-fronts-slowed-ehr-purchases-insights-from-klass-2026-acute-care-ehr-market-share-report/10709/#:~:text=KLAS%20validated%20net-new%20EHR,Oracle%20Health%2C%20and%20TruBridge)

### 13.3. Methodological Appendices

- Source hierarchy and weighting: The report used a four tier evidence hierarchy. Tier one included audited annual reports, SEC filings, official investor releases, and regulator publications. Tier two included KLAS reports and official vendor announcements. Tier three included reputable trade and financial press. Tier four included academic preprints and commercial market research used for context rather than for core issuer level facts.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf) [36](https://www.nexus-ehealth.com/company/investor-relations/financial-reports?cid=115869&file=files%2Fnexus-ag%2FUnternehmen%2FInvestor+Relations%2FFinanzberichte%2F2024%2FNEXUS+Annual+Report+2024.pdf#:~:text=NEXUS%20at%20a%20glance,%2F%20Annual%20Report%202024) [26](https://klasresearch.com/report/2026-best-in-klas-awards-software-and-services/3906#:~:text=A%20Best%20in%20KLAS,top%20vendors%20should%20provide)
- Time boundary: All sourcing was constrained to the most current information available through mid 2026, with explicit preference for FY2025 company reporting, 2026 KLAS publications, and 2026 regulatory or transaction updates. Older sources were retained only where they remained the latest reliable disclosure for private companies or long cycle market structures.[235](https://d1f19qmytqk9eo.cloudfront.net/edgar0105/2026/06/22/1341439/000119312526277521/document/orcl-20260531.htm) [157](https://engage.klasresearch.com/press-release/epic-athenahealth-chartis-optimum-healthcare-it-and-impact-advisors-win-2026-overall-best-in-klasawards/#:~:text=February%204%2C%202026%20%E2%80%93,Services%20report%2C%20released%20today)
- Peer set construction: The benchmark universe combined global acute care leaders, ambulatory cloud leaders, and European regional incumbents. Inclusion was based on one or more of the following criteria: meaningful hospital or ambulatory market share, relevance in DACH or Western Europe, strategic overlap with CGM in ambulatory, hospital, pharmacy, or interoperability workflows, or significance in current M&A and customer satisfaction discussions.[117](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ehrs/hospital-ehr-market-share-leaders-in-2025-by-category/) [343](https://www.dedalus.com/anz/media/news/dedalus-ranked-1-in-2026-best-in-klas-software-services-for-acute-care-ehr-in-dach-and-southern-europe/#:~:text=6th%20of%20February%202026,Europe%20%28mainly%20Spain%29%20regions) [36](https://www.nexus-ehealth.com/company/investor-relations/financial-reports?cid=115869&file=files%2Fnexus-ag%2FUnternehmen%2FInvestor+Relations%2FFinanzberichte%2F2024%2FNEXUS+Annual+Report+2024.pdf#:~:text=NEXUS%20at%20a%20glance,%2F%20Annual%20Report%202024)
- Financial normalization: Revenue, EBITDA, recurring revenue, and R&D figures were reported in issuer disclosed currencies and periods, then compared on a like for like basis where possible. No synthetic margin or valuation estimates were created for private vendors unless a reputable source explicitly provided them. Where segment definitions differed, the report favored directional comparability over forced precision.[100](https://www.cgm.com/_Resources/Persistent/61f7dd414a24fb4aceb42adb325d6c95176ab547/260305%20CGM%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf) [36](https://www.nexus-ehealth.com/company/investor-relations/financial-reports?cid=115869&file=files%2Fnexus-ag%2FUnternehmen%2FInvestor+Relations%2FFinanzberichte%2F2024%2FNEXUS+Annual+Report+2024.pdf#:~:text=NEXUS%20at%20a%20glance,%2F%20Annual%20Report%202024)
- Treatment of private company opacity: For Epic, athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, MEDITECH, and Dedalus, the report separated disclosed facts from inferred positioning. Financial scale, valuation, and margin comparisons were only included when supported by official company statements or high credibility external reporting. Otherwise, benchmarking relied on market share, KLAS standing, product breadth, and strategic role.[135](https://www.eclinicalworks.com/about-us/) [25](https://www.athenahealth.com/about/awards/best-in-klas-recognitions#:~:text=Image%3A%202026%20KLAS%20Award,%26%20Services%2C%E2%80%9D%20February%202026) [239](https://www.forbes.com/sites/katiejennings/2024/09/30/epic-systems-billionaire-judy-faulkner-private-independent-never-ipo/)
- Market share methodology: U.S. acute care share references were based on KLAS derived public summaries and were interpreted as directional indicators of installed base concentration rather than exact universal census counts. Non U.S. hospital positioning relied on KLAS global decision data and regional vendor disclosures, especially where bed counts and facility counts were used as proxies for procurement gravity.[117](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ehrs/hospital-ehr-market-share-leaders-in-2025-by-category/) [363](https://www.dedalus.com/uki/media/news/dedalus-records-the-highest-number-of-contracted-hospital-beds-for-the-fourth-consecutive-year-according-to-klas-research)
- Customer satisfaction methodology: KLAS awards, category scores, and migration commentary were used as the principal independent satisfaction and loyalty proxies because directly comparable public NRR, GRR, or churn disclosures are largely unavailable across the peer set. Where no suite level KLAS evidence existed for a vendor, the report explicitly noted the visibility gap rather than imputing a satisfaction ranking.[26](https://klasresearch.com/report/2026-best-in-klas-awards-software-and-services/3906#:~:text=A%20Best%20in%20KLAS,top%20vendors%20should%20provide) [343](https://www.dedalus.com/anz/media/news/dedalus-ranked-1-in-2026-best-in-klas-software-services-for-acute-care-ehr-in-dach-and-southern-europe/#:~:text=6th%20of%20February%202026,Europe%20%28mainly%20Spain%29%20regions)
- Valuation methodology: Public market multiples were calculated from contemporaneous market capitalization or enterprise value snapshots and the latest available annual revenue or EBITDA disclosures. Oracle was treated only as a partial reference because its multiple reflects a diversified software and cloud platform rather than Oracle Health as a standalone asset.[431](https://www.marketscreener.com/quote/stock/COMPUGROUP-MEDICAL-SE-CO--191076936/) [421](https://companiesmarketcap.com/nexus-ag/marketcap/) [404](https://www.financecharts.com/stocks/ORCL/summary/enterprise-value) [235](https://d1f19qmytqk9eo.cloudfront.net/edgar0105/2026/06/22/1341439/000119312526277521/document/orcl-20260531.htm)
