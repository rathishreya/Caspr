## Executive Summary

The Market Size and Growth section of the report addresses the anticipated robust growth of the U.S. electric vehicle (EV) charging infrastructure market. The industry is expected to expand from $5.92 billion in 2025 to $36.99 billion by 2033, with a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 25.7% from 2026 to 2033. This significant expansion is largely driven by the increase in public charging ports, estimated at approximately 312,486 by mid-2026. Fast charging is highlighted as a key growth area, with public fast-charging sessions projected to rise to 141 million in 2025, marking a 30% year-over-year increase, and the addition of 18,000 new DC fast-charging ports anticipated in the same year.

The Competitive Landscape section outlines the fragmented nature of the EV fast-charging market, which accommodates over 120 operators. Despite this fragmentation, a few national brands, particularly Tesla, dominate the market. In 2025, Tesla accounted for 37.6% of all new U.S. public fast-charging deployments, with competitors like EVgo, Electrify America, ChargePoint, and the emerging Ionna network trailing behind. Growth in the market is also being driven by nontraditional hosts such as Walmart.

In the Policy and Regulatory Environment section, the report examines federal initiatives supporting EV infrastructure, backed by a $7.5 billion national framework from the IIJA/BIL, encompassing the NEVI and CFI programs. However, challenges persist due to slow resource deployment and a less favorable regulatory environment, marked by setbacks such as the rescission of the 2009 Endangerment Finding and delays related to California's waiver and Advanced Clean Cars rules.

The Profitability and Business Model Challenges section describes the profitability difficulties within the sector, attributable to high fixed costs and uneven utilization. Despite increasing asset productivity, companies like ChargePoint reported adjusted EBITDA losses in fiscal 2026. Strategies to address these challenges include increasing usage, improving uptime, and diversifying revenue streams beyond traditional charging fees.

Finally, the Long-Term Outlook section projects a 14% CAGR through 2040 for the U.S. public DC fast-charging segment, aiming for a future with improved utilization and profitability amid ongoing risks like grid interconnection delays and emissions policies. The growth of this segment hinges on overcoming consumer hesitance due to range anxiety and uneven charging confidence, with the market favoring operators who can effectively leverage policy opportunities and ensure high utilization of grid-ready stations.

## 2. Competitive Landscape

The market remains fragmented, with DOE’s charging-network registry listing well over 120 operators and service providers, but scale is still concentrated in a handful of national brands.[7](https://afdc.energy.gov/stations/charging-networks) Tesla remained the clear deployment leader in 2025, adding 6,786 Supercharger ports and accounting for 37.6% of all new U.S. public fast-charging deployments that year; Paren says Tesla added more ports than the next nine operators combined.[5](https://www.paren.app/reports/state-of-the-industry-report-us-ev-fast-charging-full-year-2025) The next tier includes EVgo, Electrify America, ChargePoint, and the rapidly emerging Ionna network, which GM said had already reached 120+ locations nationwide by July 2026, while Q2 2026 market tracking also showed Walmart and other nontraditional hosts accelerating deployments.[8](https://news.gm.com/home.detail.html/Pages/topic/us/en/2026/jul/0710-ev-drivers-charge-120-ionna-locations.html)[9](https://evwire.com/p/us-ev-fast-charging-network-q2-2026-paren-report) The near-term takeaway is a market that is still structurally fragmented, but with Tesla retaining a decisive lead in throughput, brand trust, and deployment velocity.[5](https://www.paren.app/reports/state-of-the-industry-report-us-ev-fast-charging-full-year-2025)

## 3. Policy and Regulatory Environment

Federal support remains large on paper: the IIJA/BIL created a $7.5 billion national EV charging buildout framework, including nearly $5 billion for NEVI and $2.5 billion for CFI, with additional support mechanisms layered through the IRA and related federal programs.[10](https://driveelectric.gov/files/nevi-annual-report-2023-2024.pdf)[11](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R47675/R47675.2.pdf) Deployment has lagged appropriations, however; GAO reported that only 384 NEVI- and CFI-funded chargers were open to the public as of April 2025 and warned that federal agencies lacked strong enough performance management to track outcomes effectively.[12](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-106992) The regulatory backdrop has also turned less supportive in 2026, with EPA finalizing rescission of the 2009 Endangerment Finding and related vehicle GHG standards, while California waiver and ACC-related rules have been pushed back into congressional and federal review.[13](https://www.epa.gov/regulations-emissions-vehicles-and-engines/final-rule-rescission-greenhouse-gas-endangerment)[14](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/epa-fulfills-statutory-obligation-transmitting-four-california-waiver-rules-congress) For operators and investors, the headline is a widening gap between still-material public funding availability and a more uncertain federal-state regulatory trajectory.[10](https://driveelectric.gov/files/nevi-annual-report-2023-2024.pdf)[12](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-106992)

## 4. Profitability and Business Model Challenges

Profitability remains the sector’s central challenge because utilization is still uneven and networks carry high fixed costs across hardware, interconnection, maintenance, software, and customer support, even as session growth is improving asset productivity.[5](https://www.paren.app/reports/state-of-the-industry-report-us-ev-fast-charging-full-year-2025)[15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.09582) Public-company disclosures show the pressure clearly: ChargePoint was still reporting an adjusted EBITDA loss in fiscal 2026, while EVgo continues to frame late 2026 as the start of a profitability inflection rather than a completed transition.[16](https://www.chargepoint.com/about/news/chargepoint-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-fiscal-year-2026-financial-results)[17](https://investors.evgo.com/static-files/553d5f22-e338-444c-89aa-00d0fc2a6979) The operating levers are increasingly clear—higher utilization, better uptime, easier payments, and lower service-call intensity—while monetization is broadening beyond charging fees into software, fleet services, advertising, and grid or energy-management services.[16](https://www.chargepoint.com/about/news/chargepoint-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-fiscal-year-2026-financial-results)[18](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1777393/000177739326000024/fy26chptars.pdf) For industry professionals, the practical read-through is that scale alone is not enough; reliability and customer experience are becoming the main route to EBITDA durability.[5](https://www.paren.app/reports/state-of-the-industry-report-us-ev-fast-charging-full-year-2025)[17](https://investors.evgo.com/static-files/553d5f22-e338-444c-89aa-00d0fc2a6979)

## 5. Long-Term Outlook

The long-range buildout case remains positive despite policy volatility: Wood Mackenzie expects the U.S. public DC fast-charging segment to grow at a 14% CAGR through 2040, reaching about 475,000 ports and roughly $3.3 billion in annual market value.[19](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/public-ev-fast-charging-segment-poised-for-robust-growth-woodmac/758006/)[20](https://eu-assets.contentstack.com/v3/assets/blt452efb5f3e725f7e/blteb245ac55db12c68/68d275f2923130eb3d1f5aca/EV_Infrastructure_News_Report_2025_Digital.pdf) That outlook assumes utilization improves enough for profitability to emerge by the middle of the next decade, but execution risk remains tied to grid interconnection delays, capital discipline, and whether public funding offsets a tougher emissions-policy environment.[12](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-106992)[13](https://www.epa.gov/regulations-emissions-vehicles-and-engines/final-rule-rescission-greenhouse-gas-endangerment)[19](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/public-ev-fast-charging-segment-poised-for-robust-growth-woodmac/758006/) Consumer adoption is still a gating factor because charging confidence remains uneven; while the exact share varies by survey, range anxiety and charging access continue to rank among the top barriers to EV adoption in U.S. market research.[2](https://evcablehub.co.uk/blogs/journal/ev-charging-statistics-us)[19](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/public-ev-fast-charging-segment-poised-for-robust-growth-woodmac/758006/) Net-net, the market still points to structural expansion, but the winners are likely to be operators that can combine policy capture, grid-ready siting, and consistently reliable high-utilization assets.[19](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/public-ev-fast-charging-segment-poised-for-robust-growth-woodmac/758006/)
