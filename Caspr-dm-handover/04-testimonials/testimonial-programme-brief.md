## 1 · Why this is worth doing properly

Caspr's single largest conversion barrier is documented and unambiguous: *"will AI-generated research be credible enough to put in front of a board, an investor, or a client?"* Every ICP raises it. No amount of copy answers it.

What answers it is a person with twenty years at Kantar, Nielsen, GfK, Euromonitor, IDC or S&P Global saying, on camera, in their own words, that the output holds up.

We have access to sixteen such people. The marginal cost is a video-call recording and an editor's time. **This is the highest-leverage asset available to the company at close to zero cost**, and it is currently unbuilt.

So the standard is: produce this to the quality of the product. A grainy webcam clip with laptop audio undermines the exact claim it is making. A properly lit, properly recorded, properly graded interview with a named senior practitioner does the opposite.

**Build once, use for two years.** Ten interviews at 30–45 minutes each yields a library of roughly 200 usable segments — website, ads, sales decks, email, social, the `/customers` page, ICP landing pages, and the sales objection-handling pack. Only a fraction gets used at launch. The rest is inventory.

## 2 · The boundary rule — read before any outreach

**No Caspr-published material names Ghost Research.** Not in the video, not in the lower third, not in the caption, not in the outreach email, not in the consent form. This is a standing instruction and it is not negotiable at production level.

The distinction to hold:

| | |
|---|---|
| **How we reach them** | Through the existing expert-relations SPOC and their existing relationship. Entirely fine, and the reason this is cheap |
| **How they are published** | As independent senior practitioners in their own field, attributed to their own career credentials |

**Attribution format — use exactly this shape:**

> **Andrea Peltrini**
> 18 years in consumer intelligence · formerly Euromonitor International

**Never:**
- Any wording that positions Caspr as a way to bypass expert validation
- Any suggestion the speaker is employed by, or speaking for, another Caspr-adjacent entity
- Any on-screen logo, badge or lower third other than Caspr's

**Interview discipline:** if a participant mentions a specific engagement, client, or the organisation through which they encountered Caspr, let them finish — do not interrupt — and flag the timecode for the editor to cut. Interrupting produces a stilted interview; a flagged timecode costs nothing.

## 4 · Participant selection

Sixteen available. Record **twelve to yield ten** — expect two to be unusable for reasons outside anyone's control (connection, environment, or simply not being comfortable on camera).

### Selection criteria, in priority order

1. **Sector spread.** Do not record ten consumer-goods researchers. Target coverage across consumer/FMCG, technology, financial services, energy, healthcare, industrial, and retail — so ICP landing pages and sector campaigns each have a matching face.
2. **Firm recognition.** Kantar, Nielsen, GfK, Euromonitor, IDC, S&P Global and MarketsandMarkets are the names a sceptical buyer recognises instantly. Weight toward these.
3. **Depth of actual Caspr use.** Someone who has worked with real output speaks in specifics. Someone who has seen a demo speaks in adjectives, and adjectives are useless.
4. **Geographic spread.** Supports the multilingual argument (§9.4) and the non-English market opening.
5. **Comfort on camera.** The SPOC will know. A brilliant researcher who freezes on camera is a written testimonial, not a video one — and that is a perfectly good outcome, not a failure.

### Language

**Participants may record in their working language.** A subtitled testimonial in Spanish, Portuguese or Russian is not a compromise — it is a distinct asset for a market where Caspr has no presence and native-language generation is a real differentiator. Aim for **at least two non-English** among the ten.

## 5 · Recruitment

### The ask — what the SPOC actually sends

Short, specific, and honest about the time cost. Do not send a marketing email to a senior practitioner.

> **Subject:** 30 minutes on camera — your view on the research output
>
> [Name],
>
> You have worked with the analysis Caspr produces. We would like to record a short conversation about it — what it does well, where you would push back, and how it compares to how this work has been done for the last twenty years.
>
> Thirty to forty minutes on a video call. No script and no approval of what you say — if you have criticisms, those are the parts we most want on record. We will send three or four topics in advance so nothing is a surprise.
>
> We will handle the recording quality end to end; you need a quiet room and a good connection. You will receive the finished piece and are free to use it yourself.
>
> Would a slot in the next fortnight work?

**Why "no approval of what you say" is in the email:** it is the difference between a testimonial and an advertisement, and senior practitioners can tell instantly. It also happens to be the legal position — endorsements must reflect genuine opinion. Participants get approval over *use*, not over *content*.

### Follow-up cadence

Initial ask → reminder at day 4 → personal note from Joy at day 10 for non-responders in the top-six target list. Stop after three touches.

### Consent and release — before recording, not after

A countersigned release is required before the session. It must cover:

- Perpetual, worldwide, royalty-free use across website, social, paid advertising, sales material, email and press
- Right to edit, subtitle, translate and excerpt
- Use of name, job title, career history and likeness
- **The right to withdraw** with 30 days' notice, and a commitment to remove from owned channels — this is what makes senior people comfortable signing, and it costs us very little
- Confirmation the views are their own genuine opinion and that no payment was made for a favourable view

If any participant is paid or compensated in any way, that must be disclosed on the published asset. Cleanest outcome: do not compensate. A finished professional-grade video of themselves is the consideration, and it is genuinely valuable to them.

## 6 · Technical setup — participant instructions

Send this as a **one-page PDF** 48 hours before the session, plus a 10-minute technical check call 24 hours before. **The check call is mandatory.** It is the difference between ten usable recordings and six.

### 6.1 Recording platform

**Use a local-recording platform — Riverside.fm or equivalent — not a standard video call.**

This is the single most important technical decision in the brief. Zoom, Meet and Teams transmit heavily compressed video; whatever is captured is capped at that compressed quality forever. Local-recording platforms record full-quality video **on each participant's own device** and upload it separately, so the finished file is unaffected by connection quality during the call.

- **Record at 4K where the participant's camera supports it.** A 4K master allows a punch-in crop in the edit that reads as a second camera angle (§8.3) — this is how a single-camera remote interview gets cutaways
- Separate audio and video tracks per participant
- Local backup recording enabled on both ends
- Interviewer also records — their audio is needed even though they never appear on screen

### 6.2 Camera

| | Instruction |
|---|---|
| **Preferred** | A recent phone, mounted horizontally on a small tripod or propped on books. Phone cameras substantially outperform laptop webcams |
| **Acceptable** | An external USB webcam (1080p or better) |
| **Avoid** | The built-in laptop webcam, unless nothing else exists |
| **Height** | Lens at **eye level**. A laptop on a desk points up the nose; raise it on a box or stack of books |
| **Distance** | Roughly an arm's length plus a little — chest and head in frame |
| **Framing** | Eyes on the upper third line. A small gap of headroom, never a crop through the top of the head. Centre or slightly off-centre; consistent across the session |
| **Stability** | Fixed. No handheld, no motion |
| **Orientation** | **Horizontal.** The vertical cuts are made in the edit from the 4K master |

### 6.3 Lighting

| | Instruction |
|---|---|
| **Best** | Sit **facing a window**, daylight on the face, no direct sun |
| **Never** | A window or bright lamp **behind** — it silhouettes the subject and cannot be fixed in the edit |
| **Never** | Overhead ceiling light as the only source — it puts shadows under the eyes |
| **If no daylight** | Two lamps at 45° either side of the camera, roughly level with the face, or a ring light at eye level |
| **Consistency** | Curtains partly closed if the sun moves during a 40-minute session |

### 6.4 Audio — more important than the picture

Viewers forgive a soft image. They do not forgive bad sound, and bad sound reads as low production value more than anything else on this list.

| | Instruction |
|---|---|
| **Best** | Any USB microphone, positioned just out of frame |
| **Good** | Wired earphones with an inline microphone |
| **Acceptable** | Modern wireless earbuds |
| **Avoid** | The laptop's built-in microphone |
| **Room** | A room with soft furnishing — curtains, rug, sofa, books. Avoid glass-walled meeting rooms and empty hard-floored rooms; the echo is unfixable |
| **Silence** | Phone on silent and face down. Notifications off on the computer. Air conditioning and fans **off** — they produce a hum that is inaudible live and obvious in the edit |
| **Test** | Thirty seconds recorded and played back on the check call |

### 6.5 Background

| | Instruction |
|---|---|
| **Use** | A real room. Depth behind the subject — a bookshelf, a wall with one piece of art, an office interior |
| **Never** | A virtual or blurred background. The edge artefacts around hair and shoulders are visible at any resolution and read as cheap. This is a hard rule |
| **Avoid** | A blank white wall directly behind — flat and lifeless |
| **Avoid** | Anything with a legible third-party logo, or clutter that dates the footage |

### 6.6 Wardrobe

Solid mid-tone colours. Avoid tight patterns, stripes and small checks — they produce moiré on video. Avoid pure white, and avoid visible branding. What they would wear to a client meeting is exactly right.

### 6.7 Connection and session hygiene

- Wired ethernet if available; otherwise close to the router
- Close every other application; VPNs off where policy allows
- Log in **10 minutes early** for a level check
- Allow **60 minutes** in the diary for a 30–40 minute recording

## 7 · The interview

### 7.1 Direction to the interviewer

Six rules. They determine whether the footage is usable.

1. **Answers must stand alone.** The interviewer is never heard in the final cut, so every answer has to make sense without the question. Brief the participant explicitly: *"Please fold my question into your answer — instead of 'yes, about three weeks', say 'the work that used to take about three weeks…'"* Remind them once mid-session if they drift.
2. **Ask for the specific.** "It saves a lot of time" is unusable. "It saves time" → *"Can you put a number on that? What did that particular piece of work take before?"* Numbers, timeframes, sector names, document lengths.
3. **Let silence run.** After an answer ends, wait three seconds. The second thing people say is almost always better than the first, and interviewers destroy more good material by filling silence than by any other habit.
4. **Ask for the criticism, and mean it.** A testimonial with no reservation reads as bought. *"Where does it fall short? What would you not use it for?"* An expert saying "I would not present this without reading it myself, but the structure and the sourcing are sound" is far more persuasive than unqualified praise, and it is exactly the register the brand uses.
5. **Never supply the words.** Give the topic, never the sentence. Beyond being the legal position on endorsements, a coached line is audible.
6. **Re-ask, don't correct.** If an answer is good but rambling: *"That's useful — could you give me the short version of that in one or two sentences?"* Participants find this normal and it produces the cut-down soundbite.
7. **Never lead them to a competitor comparison — but never stop one either.** Do not ask how Caspr compares to ChatGPT, to any chatbot, or to AI in general. Naming a competitor puts Caspr in that competitor's category, which is precisely what the positioning avoids. **If a participant raises it unprompted, let them run** — a senior practitioner drawing the distinction in their own words is one of the most valuable things this programme can capture, and it is usable in category-education material. The difference is who introduced it.

### 7.2 Question set

Grouped by the asset each group produces. Not all questions to all participants — follow the conversation, but leave with at least one strong answer per group.

**Group A — Establishing authority** *(produces the opening of every cut)*
1. Tell me who you are and what you have spent your career doing.
2. How long have you been doing this kind of research, and for which firms?
3. What does a serious piece of market research actually involve — what does a client not see?

**Group B — The before** *(produces the problem framing)*
4. Before tools like this, how long did a piece of sector analysis take, start to finish?
5. What part of that work was genuinely analytical, and what part was assembly?
6. What did that cost a client, roughly?

**Group C — The output** *(the core credibility material — spend most of the time here)*
7. Describe the first time you saw a full Caspr analysis. What did you expect, and what did you get?
8. You know this subject better than the machine does. Where did it hold up, and where did you have to correct it?
9. How does the sourcing compare to how you would have sourced it yourself? Were there sources in it you would not have reached?
9a. When sources disagreed on a number, what did it do? Could you see how it got to the figure it used?
10. If a client had put this in front of you as a piece of commissioned work, what would you have said about it?
11. Would you be comfortable with output like this informing a decision you were advising on?

**Group D — The reservation** *(do not skip — this is what makes the rest credible)*
12. Where does it fall short?
13. What would you not use it for?
14. What should someone understand before relying on it?

**Group E — What it actually is** *(the identity and promise, in a practitioner's voice — **no competitor is named, by you or in the prompt**)*
15. If you had to describe Caspr to another researcher in one sentence, what would you say it is?
16. Is this a tool, or is it closer to a colleague? What is the honest answer?
17. Whose work does it do — and whose does it not?
18. How did you feel taking this into a client conversation, compared to work you had assembled yourself?
19. What changes for a research professional when something like this exists?

**Group F — The close** *(produces the short social cuts)*
20. In one or two sentences, what is your honest assessment?
21. Who should be using this, and who should not?

### 7.3 Session shape

| Minutes | |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Level check, small talk on camera — this is the warm-up and it is also usable B-roll of a relaxed face |
| 5–10 | Group A |
| 10–15 | Group B |
| 15–30 | Group C |
| 30–35 | Group D |
| 35–42 | Groups E and F |
| 42–45 | *"Anything I should have asked and didn't?"* — reliably produces one of the best answers of the session |

## 8 · Editorial and brand treatment

The look is the same one the existing output-showcase brief establishes: dark, quiet, confident. **No music beds, no swooshes, no kinetic type.**

### 8.1 Tokens — exact values, from the locked palette

| Element | Value |
|---|---|
| Background / letterbox | `#0C0B09` (`--dark-surface-0`) |
| Primary text on dark | `#F5F4F0` (`--dark-text-primary`) |
| Secondary text on dark | `#9C9A94` (`--dark-text-secondary`) |
| Accent | `#E8453C` — this exact red. Not `#EF4444`, not `#FF4444` |
| Divider rules | `#2E2C28` (`--dark-border`), 1px |

### 8.2 Typography

| Use | Font | Spec |
|---|---|---|
| Pull-quote cards | **Instrument Serif** Regular 400 | Never below 32px. It loses its character and reads as a cheap web font |
| Name in lower third | **Inter** Medium | 20px, `#F5F4F0` |
| Credential line | **Inter** Regular | 14px, `#9C9A94` |
| Numbers and stats | **DM Mono** Regular | Numbers only, never prose |
| Subtitles | **Inter** Regular | 18px, `#F5F4F0`, 60% opacity black plate behind |

### 8.3 Grade and framing

- Neutral, slightly warm grade. Lift the blacks a touch — crushed blacks read as amateur correction
- Match skin tone across all ten so the library cuts together as one set
- **The punch-in crop is the second camera.** From the 4K master, cut between a wide (full 4K frame, scaled) and a ~1.4× punch-in on the face. Use the punch-in on emotional or emphatic lines, the wide elsewhere. This is what removes the static-webcam feel
- Cut on the punch-in to hide edit points where an answer has been tightened. Never leave a visible jump cut on a static frame

### 8.4 The lower third

Appears once, 3 seconds after the speaker starts, holds 4 seconds, fades. Never returns in the same cut.

```
ANDREA PELTRINI                          ← Inter Medium 20px, #F5F4F0
18 years in consumer intelligence        ← Inter Regular 14px, #9C9A94
Formerly Euromonitor International       ← Inter Regular 14px, #9C9A94
```

A 1px rule in `#E8453C` at 40% opacity animates left to right beneath the name over 0.6s. Subtle — a whisper, not a graphic statement.

### 8.5 Cutaways

Cut away to the actual analysis being discussed: report pages, a table of contents, a data table, a citation red dot expanding to show its source. Screen-recorded from real output, never mocked up. This ties the testimonial to the product and reuses the visual language of the existing showcase film.

### 8.6 Openings and endings

- **Open on the speaker, mid-thought.** No title card, no logo animation, no "we asked experts…" framing. The first thing on screen is a credible person talking
- **End on the wordmark**, 1.5 seconds, on `#0C0B09`. Nothing else. No CTA burned into the master — CTAs are added per-placement so the master stays reusable

## 10 · Quality control

The editor delivers against this checklist. **Any single failure sends the cut back.**

| | Check |
|---|---|
| ☐ | Audio: no hum, no echo, no clipping. Normalised to −16 LUFS |
| ☐ | No jump cut visible on a static frame |
| ☐ | Lower third appears once, correct fonts, correct hex values |
| ☐ | Accent red is exactly `#E8453C` |
| ☐ | Instrument Serif never used below 32px |
| ☐ | No exclamation points anywhere on screen |
| ☐ | No banned vocabulary in titles, captions or subtitles — *platform · leverages · algorithms · workflows · powerful AI · revolutionary · game-changing · chatbot · web scraping · hallucinate* |
| ☐ | **"LAM" / "Large Analysis Model" does not appear** |
| ☐ | **No competitor or AI product is named** in any title, caption, subtitle or quote card — including in a participant's own quoted words when that quote is used as a headline. Such quotes may be used inside category-education material, never as a lead line |
| ☐ | **No caption or quote card asks the viewer to do work** — no "verify", "check the sources", "see for yourself". The evidence is shown, never assigned |
| ☐ | Source count stated as **25M+** if stated at all |
| ☐ | **No reference to Ghost Research** in any frame, caption, subtitle, file name or metadata |
| ☐ | No third-party logo visible in frame or background |
| ☐ | Credential line factually correct and matched to the participant's own record |
| ☐ | Signed release on file before publication |
| ☐ | Subtitles present and burned in on vertical and square cuts |
| ☐ | Punch-in used at least twice — the cut is not a static webcam frame throughout |

**Joy approves the first two edits before the remaining eight are cut.** Fixing a treatment decision once at interview two is cheap; discovering it at interview ten is not.

## 13 · What this unblocks

For the record, since three separate pieces of work have been waiting on it:

- **`site-truth.md` §7** — every testimonial and logo currently on the live site is invented. Attributed quotes from named people on a public page are a regulated claim in the US, UK and EU. This programme replaces them with real, consented, documented ones
- **The paid-acquisition gate** in `gtm-strategy.md` — "first testimonial collected" is a stated precondition for LinkedIn ad spend
- **The `/customers` page** — live, orphaned, and empty of substance
