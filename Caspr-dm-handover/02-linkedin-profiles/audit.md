## 2 · Eight findings, ordered by value against cost to fix

### 2.1 Four spellings of the Caspr entity across seven profiles — fix first

`Ghost Research` (×2) · `Caspr. Research` (×2) · `Caspr.` (×2) · `Caspr.ai` (×1).

Set aside the Ghost entries, which are a deliberate choice (§2.2): the five Caspr people still list the company three different ways. A prospect checking the team sees three entities, and mechanically it splits the LinkedIn Company Page follower graph three ways, so no page accumulates authority and none of the profiles feeds the page we advertise from.

**Action: one canonical Caspr company page, selected identically from the dropdown** — so it links rather than free-texts — by Jayant, Dixit, Amit, Keshav, Naman and Kartikey.

### 2.2 Dual-brand on Joy's profile only — decided

**Joy carries both.** Ghost is being built as a brand in parallel, and the 22k audience is the right place for both to be visible. **Everyone else on the core team tags to Caspr.** Ghost-side experts, including part-time ones, are tagged to Ghost — a separate workstream, not covered here. *(Joy, 2026-08-19.)*

Mechanics that matter, because LinkedIn handles this badly by default:

- LinkedIn shows **one** current position in the profile intro card. It picks by position order, not recency. **Set Caspr as the displayed position** — Ghost remains listed and fully visible in Experience, it simply is not the line under the name.
- Keep both as **concurrent current roles** with honest start dates. Do not end-date Ghost.
- The **headline** is the one line that carries everywhere — every comment, every search result, every post impression. Joy's is currently brand-neutral (`Serial Founder & CEO, ex-Mckinsey`), which is why the dual arrangement works. Keep it close to neutral rather than trying to name both companies in it; a headline naming two companies reads as a portfolio, not a founder.

**Jayant is the open item.** His profile also reads Ghost Research, and unlike Joy's it carries no brand-neutral headline to hold both. Per the rule above he tags to Caspr — confirm, since he is a founder of both.

### 2.2a The one-minute fix that matters most

Whatever is decided on ordering, **Joy's profile currently shows Ghost Research as the sole employer.** Every profile visit generated by a Caspr post lands on a page that never mentions Caspr. Adding the Caspr position and setting it to display is the highest-leverage minute in the entire GTM.

### 2.3 `ex-Mckinsey` is misspelled

It is `McKinsey`. It sits in the headline of the person whose McKinsey pedigree is the documented answer to the #1 objection in the funnel (`gtm-strategy.md` §12). Free to fix, and exactly the class of detail this brand cannot afford to get wrong.

### 2.4 Jayant's headline is the biggest wasted asset in the plan

`Polymath | Mathematics Geek | Data Science | Machine Learning | Deep Learning | Research` describes a job applicant, not the co-founder of an Analytical AI company. No company, no role, no reason to follow.

The GTM has been looking for a credibility voice that is not Joy's. It already exists: Jayant's own ML-paper critique drew 34 reactions and 4 comments on 2,454 followers — proportionally stronger than most posts on the larger account. **The builder's voice works. It is simply not switched on.**

### 2.5 Keshav's headline still says EZ

`Junior Data Scientist at EZ` while the company field says Caspr. A year stale. The honours list behind it — hackathon wins, Leetcode count — reads as a graduate job hunt rather than a member of a founding team.

He shows as a **2nd-degree connection** — Joy and he are not connected. His sole recent activity is resharing a "67+ interview questions for 40 LPA offers" list, which reads publicly as job-seeking. His headline carries no company at all.

Two actions: connect internally, and give him a lane so his feed says *frontend engineer building something* rather than *frontend engineer looking*.

### 2.7 Naman has never posted

766 followers, three congratulation comments in eleven months. Do not start here. Naman goes on **comments only** for the first eight weeks and builds a base before being asked to publish.

### 2.8 The Caspr company page is publishing prohibited copy right now

Live posts on the Caspr page, reposted by team members, contain:

- **"Large Analysis Model (LAM)"** — explicitly banned in `CLAUDE.md` and `site-truth.md` §7

## 3 · The format that already works — proven on your own accounts

Two data points, both from this team, both pointing the same way.

| Post | Format | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Joy — AI Summit strategic summary | Real event → Caspr analysis → **93-page document attached** → "Put Caspr to work" | **2,945 impressions, 40 reactions** — the best Caspr-related post on the account |

The `gtm-strategy.md` §9 thesis that 40% of content should be output showcases is not a hypothesis. It is the best-performing thing either founder has published. And the Jayant post demonstrates the missing half: **good content with no coordinated engagement dies.** That is precisely what the seven-person layer is for.

One further signal: Joy's highest-reach post of the year — 9,091 impressions — was the capped WhatsApp group for CXOs and investors. Pure utility, no product. **Utility outperforms opinion on this account by roughly 3×**, which argues for tools, datasets and access as content, not just commentary.

## 5 · Voice lanes — who owns what

Never two people on the same subject in the same week. Each lane is a standing beat, so the calendar writes itself.

| Person | Lane | Cadence | Never |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Joy** | **The analyst.** Findings from real analyses, the category argument, ICP pain, customer stories | 1/week | Technical architecture; generic AI commentary |
| **Jayant** | **The builder.** How Caspr sources live and keeps a corpus current, why citation is hard, evaluation, security, native multilingual generation, honest engineering trade-offs | 1/week | Marketing claims; pricing |
| **Dixit** | **The applied scientist.** Retrieval, evaluation, hallucination measurement, agent design | 1/fortnight | Product roadmap |
| **Amit** | **The engineer at work.** What shipped, what broke, what the team learned | 1/fortnight | Strategy; positioning |
| **Kartikey** | **The interface.** Designing for long documents, citation UI, reading experience, performance | 1/fortnight | Anything speaking for the company |
| **Keshav** | **The learner.** Genuinely useful technical notes from inside the build | 1/fortnight | Anything speaking for the company |
| **Naman** | **Comments only** for eight weeks, then joins the engineer lane | — | — |

### The engagement rule — the part that carries the reach

LinkedIn weights substantive comments in the first 30–60 minutes heavily. Seven people commenting on a founder post materially changes its distribution. This works only if it is real.

> **No coordinated applause.** Every comment must add a fact, a number, a counter-example, or a genuine question from that person's own domain. If a comment could have been written by someone who had not read the post, it does not go out.

"Great post" from five colleagues is visible astroturf, and it costs a defensibility brand more than the reach is worth.

**Weekly minimum per person:** two substantive comments — one on a team post, one on an external post in their lane (a target ICP, a community, a journalist).

The proposed total is kept; where it is spent changes.

**The core team should not be reviewing content.** Review belongs to the three-person marketing team — an experienced performance TL, a social specialist and an SEO practitioner at 2–3 hours each is 8–9 hours of qualified review capacity per week. Spending founder minutes on QA that a 15-year performance marketer does better is the wrong trade. Founder minutes buy distribution, which nobody else on the team can provide.
