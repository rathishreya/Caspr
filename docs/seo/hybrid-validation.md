# The hybrid, tested across dimensions — and the counter-case

*2026-08-25. All free tools. No API spend. Companion to [`strategy-stress-test.md`](strategy-stress-test.md).*

## 1 · Does the pattern hold, or did I test the easy cases?

**Seven query shapes now tested, across ICP, sector, geography and function.**

| # | Shape | ICP served | Who ranks | The contradiction on page one |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `copper production by country` | Category · Strategy | INN, WorldPopulationReview, **2 identical syndications** | *"Chile largest"* vs *"China 48% of refined"* — **two definitions of production** |
| 2 | `India agricultural exports` | Strategy · Corp dev | **Exporter blogs**, paywalled aggregators | `$52.55bn FY25-26` vs `$55bn by 2025` |
| 3 | `premium ready meals market UK` | Category · MR | marketresearchfuture, marknteladvisors | **`$5.86bn · 4.95%` vs `$6.46bn · 12.4%`** |
| 4 | **`SaaS revenue multiples 2026`** | **Investors** ★ | M&A advisors, boutique consultancies | **`8.5x NTM` vs `3.3x TTM`** — and `4–5.5x`, `3–12x`, `4–9x` private, from three advisors |
| 5 | **`industrial valves Saudi Arabia`** | **Corp dev · Investors** | **openpr.com — a press-release site — ranks #1**, then IMARC ×3, MarkNtel, DataBridge | **IMARC `$725.0M` vs MarkNtel `$990M`** — same market, same year, **36% apart** |
| 6 | `best market research tools` | All — **purchase intent** | Vendor listicles listing each other | Caspr absent from every list |
| 7 | **`best AI deep research tool`** | All — **purchase intent** ★ | Kimi ×3, cybernews, jotform, index.dev, theairankings | Caspr absent. **Competition is explicitly on citation reliability** |

### 1.1 The pattern holds everywhere, and it is worst where coverage is thinnest

**Shape 5 is the strongest case, not the weakest.** A niche B2B category in an emerging geography — precisely where syndicated coverage thins out — produces a **36% disagreement between two sellers** and lets **a press-release distribution site rank first**.

> **Coverage thinness and contradiction move together.** The harder the question, the worse what is published — and the more valuable a sourced, reconciled answer becomes.

### 1.2 Shape 4 is the highest-stakes version, and it is our launch ICP

`8.5x NTM` against `3.3x TTM` is not a rounding difference. **It is forward versus trailing revenue** — a definitional trap that changes a valuation by 2.5×, published by ten M&A advisors, **reconciled by none of them.**

**An analyst who takes 8.5x when the comparable is 3.3x has a materially wrong number in an IC memo.** That is `icp-personas.md:74` — *"the partner will ask where it came from"* — with real money attached.

### 1.3 Where it does NOT hold

**Methodology and education queries** (`secondary research`) — Scribbr and university libraries rank, the audience is students, and the CPC tells the story: **$9 against $300 for `competitive landscape analysis`.**

**And by extension: any query a student would plausibly ask.** That is the boundary of this strategy, and it is a real one.

## 2 · The family I had missed — AI Deep Research

*Raised by Joy, 2026-08-25. It changes the priority order.*

**The category is already sorting on the axis Caspr claims.** The AI answer for `best AI deep research tool` names **Perplexity ("most reliable citations"), ChatGPT Deep Research ("inline citations"), Kimi ("structured results with citations"), Consensus and Scite.**

> **Buyers are actively choosing an AI research tool, and they are choosing on citation quality.** That is Caspr's entire claim, and **we are absent from every list and every answer.**

**Why this outranks the data-page family on priority:**

| | Data pages | AI deep research family |
|---|---|---|
| **Intent** | Wants a number | **Wants to choose a tool** |
| **Audience risk** | Students, journalists, the curious | **Someone with a research job and a budget** |
| **Satisfies intent and leaves?** | **Yes — the core weakness** | **No — the query is the start of a purchase** |
| **Time to value** | 6 months of compounding | **Immediate on inclusion** |

### 2.1 The positioning tension, named rather than dodged

**`CLAUDE.md` rule 1: never name a competitor or the LLM category in lead copy.** This query family is *inherently* about the LLM category.

**It is compatible, and the existing tiering already resolves it:**

- **A third-party listicle is not our lead copy.** Being included in someone else's roundup is option D, and it carries no positioning cost
- **Our own pages targeting these queries live on `/vs/*`** — the sanctioned home for the generative contrast
- **The homepage never changes.** *"Not an assistant. An analyst."* stands

> **Be findable inside the category without adopting the category.** The distinction is between where we are *listed* and what we *lead with*, and the tiered copy rules already draw exactly that line.

## 3 · The counter-case, argued properly

**The instruction was to test the counter-hypothesis, not to confirm the plan. Here is the strongest version of "this does not deserve to be a major GTM channel."**

### 3.1 ⚠ Satisfied intent — the strongest argument against

**Someone searching `copper production by country` wants a number. We give them the number. They leave.**

The page has done its job and earned nothing. **This is the classic content-marketing trap, and it is worse for us than for most**, because the product's own free tier already lets them ask without an account — so even a converted visitor may never pay.

**The honest answer, and it is a design answer rather than a rebuttal:** the conversion mechanic is **not** the number, it is **the disagreement**. A reader who arrives wanting one figure and discovers that two credible sources differ by 36% now has a problem they did not know they had. **Resolving that is the product.**

**But that is a hypothesis, not a finding.** It needs testing — see T4 below — and if it fails, this family is a brand asset with poor conversion, not an acquisition channel.

### 3.2 ⚠ Wrong audience on the data pages

`copper production by country` is searched by analysts — **and by students, journalists, traders and the curious.** We have no way to tell them apart before they land, and the traffic mix could be mostly people who will never buy.

**Partial answer:** the SaaS-multiples and Saudi-valves shapes are far more buyer-specific than the commodity shape. **Not all data queries are equal, and the strategy should skew to the ones with commercial CPC** rather than treating "specific data question" as one category.

### 3.3 ⚠ AI Overviews may take the click even when we win

45% of searches show an AI Overview; clicks fall by up to 58%. **We may be cited without being visited.**

**That is a real cost and it cuts both ways** — citation is brand at the decision moment, but it is not traffic, and `x` is measured on revenue. **A channel that produces citations and no sessions will not show up in the funnel**, which means it could be working and invisible.

### 3.4 ⚠ Six months to signal, against a plan that needs signal at week four

`plan.md` reads the funnel at week 4 and `x@3` at month 3. **Search delivers neither.** If the whole GTM were leaning on it, we would be flying blind through the only period that decides whether to scale.

**This is the strongest argument for the hybrid rather than the pure play** — options D and the AI-deep-research family produce signal in weeks, not months.

### 3.5 ⚠ Thin-content risk is real and it is asymmetric

The pSEO defensibility hierarchy puts **public data at the weakest rung.** Thousands of generated pages built on public figures is the exact profile Google penalises — **and a manual action would damage the whole domain, not just those pages.**

**Mitigation is the synthesis**, which is genuinely additive rather than a reformat. **But "we think our version is additive" is what everyone penalised has said**, and the honest control is to seed narrow and watch indexation before scaling.

### 3.6 ✅ Where the counter-case fails

**It does not survive contact with shape 7.** The AI-deep-research family has none of these weaknesses: purchase intent, a buyer with a budget, immediate value on inclusion, and no dependence on ranking at all — inclusion in third-party lists is outreach, not SEO.

**And it does not survive the cost argument.** Data pages are generated from `fact_lookup` at a cached read. **The marginal cost of being wrong here is close to zero**, which is a materially different bet from committing a writer for six months.

## 4 · What survives, in priority order

| | | Why it survives the counter-case | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **AI deep research + tools listicles** — get included | **Purchase intent, right audience, immediate.** Outreach, not ranking | **Weeks** |
| **2** | **Directory listings** — G2, Datarade, Capterra | 6.5× third-party multiplier. Nearly free | **Weeks** |
| **3** | **AEO as a build standard** | Cheap now, expensive to retrofit. No downside case | Continuous |
| **4** | **Data pages, seeded narrow** — skew to **commercial-CPC shapes** (valuations, B2B niches, emerging geographies) not commodity trivia | Highest ceiling, weakest conversion evidence. **Test before scale** | **6 months** |
| **5** | **The Record** | Already funded | Ongoing |

**Dropped: methodology as a family. Dropped: `/alternatives/*` at eight pages.**

**The order inverted.** I had data pages as the prize; the counter-case and shape 7 together put the fastest, highest-intent, lowest-risk items first — and leave the big programmatic bet as **the thing we test rather than the thing we assume.**

## 5 · The one test that decides §3.1

**T4 — does the disagreement convert?**

Publish **three data pages** to the AEO standard, on commercial-CPC shapes: one valuation comparable, one B2B niche in an emerging geography, one commodity. Each shows the figure, every source, and **the disagreement stated plainly**.

**Measure:** share of visitors who reach `prompt_submitted`. **Kill condition: under 1% after 1,000 sessions**, the same bar the free tool was held to.

**If it converts, the programmatic bet is on. If it does not, these pages are a citation and brand asset — worth keeping, not worth scaling.** Either answer is worth having before we build thousands of them.
