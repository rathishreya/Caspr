**Placement:** Homepage — output showcase, above the fold or immediately below hero
**Status:** Ready for production

---

## 1. Concept

The metaphor is "The report arrives." This is not a product tour. There is no cursor, no loading bar, no workflow — only the finished document, presented as a serious piece of work. The camera moves the way a reader moves: slowly, deliberately, with the weight of someone who knows what they are looking at. Dark background, near-silence, no narration. Each shot holds long enough for the viewer to register what they are seeing — financial models, trade data tables, a 16-section table of contents — before moving to the next. The emotional register is not excitement. It is confidence. The viewer should feel as though someone just placed a thick research dossier on the desk in front of them and said nothing. That is enough.

## 3. Storyboard — Shot by Shot

### Shot 1 (0:00–0:04): The Cover

**Visual:** The PDF cover page materialises on a near-black background (#0B0B09). Industrial photography is visible — blue chemical processing towers / electrolysis equipment. The report title and Caspr logo are legible. The cover occupies approximately 75% of canvas width, centred, with a soft drop shadow creating physical weight.

**Animation:**
- Fade in over 0.8s + very subtle scale 1.03→1.00 (ease out)
- Hold 2 seconds
- Gentle slow zoom 1.00→1.04 across the hold duration
- No text overlay

**Note to editor:** The industrial photography on this specific cover grounds the video in a real industry immediately. It signals to the viewer — before a word is read — that this is about a physical production sector, not a generic business concept. Do not crop or obscure it.

### Shot 6 (0:22–0:26): The Volume

**Visual:** A rapid sequence of 8 pages from different sections of the report, flashing across the screen at 0.25s each — creating the sense that there is more depth here than can be shown in a short video.

**Page sequence:**

2. Executive Summary

## 4. Style Guide

### Background
`#0B0B09` — near-black with a warm undertone. Not pure black (#000000). The warmth prevents the video from reading as cold or corporate-tech. Consistent across all shots.

### Document Presentation
- Pages displayed at approximately 75% canvas width, centred vertically and horizontally
- Drop shadow: `0px 8px 40px rgba(0,0,0,0.6)` — creates physical weight, implies paper
- No border, no frame, no device mockup (phone, laptop, browser chrome)
- Pages should read as documents, not as software screenshots

### Typography (overlays only)
- Typeface: Inter
- Sizes: 11–14pt (never larger — this is supplementary, not headline)
- Colour: #F5F2EF (warm off-white — never pure white #FFFFFF)
- Opacity: 60–80% — always slightly transparent, never fully opaque
- Weight: Regular for metadata lines; Semi Bold for end card statements only
- No drop shadow on text overlays — the background is dark enough

### Red Accent Usage
- Colour: #E8453C (Caspr red)
- Uses: the 1px TOC rule (Shot 2), the import row highlight (Shot 3, 8% opacity), the phase accent bars (Shot 5, 50% opacity), the wordmark dot pulse (End Card)
- Never used as a bold graphic element — no red text, no red shapes, no red backgrounds
- Every red use should feel incidental, not decorative. If a red element reads as designed, reduce its opacity.

### Transitions
- Cross-dissolve only, 0.3–0.5s
- The deliberate cut between Shot 2 and Shot 3 is the single exception — it is an editorial choice, not a technical limitation
- No wipes, slides, zooms, 3D page turns, push transitions, or motion blur effects

### Pacing
- Shots 1–5: slow and deliberate. Each shot holds long enough to read
- Shot 6: intentionally fast — creates contrast and conveys volume
- End card: held long enough to read all three lines comfortably at a normal reading pace
- Total target: 26–28 seconds. Do not compress to fit under 25 seconds.

### What to Avoid
- Cursor or mouse movements of any kind
- Browser chrome, UI elements, navigation bars, or any software interface
- Typing animations or text appearing letter-by-letter
- Loading bars, progress indicators, or skeleton screens
- Upbeat, energetic, or motivational music
- Any element that reads as a product tutorial or feature demonstration
- Zoom transitions that feel like a Keynote presentation
- Colour grading that reads as warm/aspirational (golden tones, lifestyle treatment)
- Text that explains what the viewer is seeing (captions like "EBITDA Analysis" or "Implementation Roadmap")

## 6. Tool Recommendation

**Tier 1 — Professional (recommended):** Adobe After Effects.
Import exported PNGs as still images. Build one composition per shot at 1920×1080, 30fps. Use the standard Dissolve transition or opacity keyframes for cross-dissolves. The Ken Burns effect (scale + position keyframes with ease in/out) handles the slow zooms on Shot 1. Any designer with 6+ months of AE experience can execute this brief in 3–4 hours. Render to H.264 via Media Encoder. Export a separate WebM pass for web delivery.

**Tier 2 — Accessible professional:** Jitter.video (browser-based, no software install).
Import images, sequence shots with their timing controls, use built-in dissolve transitions. Jitter produces broadcast-quality MP4 output and requires no local software. Suitable for teams without AE licenses. Execution time: 4–6 hours including asset setup.

**Tier 3 — Fast:** CapCut Pro or DaVinci Resolve (free).
DaVinci Resolve: import PNGs into the timeline, set still clip duration per storyboard timings, apply Cross Dissolve transitions from the Effects Library, use Transform > Zoom for the Shot 1 scale animation. Execution time: under 2 hours. Output quality is fully adequate for web delivery at 1080p.

**Note on Figma Prototype mode:** Not suitable for video production. Figma cannot export timed compositions as MP4. Use it only for internal storyboard review if the team needs to align on visual layout before moving to a production tool.
