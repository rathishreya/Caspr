# Voice — the in-product conversation

**Proposal, 2026-08-18.** Written to slot into `brand-guidelines.md` beside
*Voice Modifications for Academic Audience*, which is the precedent: the core
voice holds, the register shifts, and the list of what does **not** change is
the part that keeps it honest.

---

## The answer in one line

**Same voice, one notch quieter — and the notch is situational awareness, not
friendliness.**

Call the register **The Analyst Between Meetings.** Same person as the one in
front of the board, same standards, lower volume, different moment.

---

## Why the shift must be *small* — smaller than instinct says

Three reasons, and the first is the one that matters commercially.

**1. The positioning caps the warmth.** *"Built for analysis, not
conversation"* is approved copy. *"Generative AI writes. Analytical AI
analyses."* is the category claim. Every generative assistant on the market is
warm, chatty and eager. **If Caspr's conversation mode sounds like one of them,
the positioning collapses at the exact moment the user is paying most
attention** — the moment Caspr is actually talking to them. Warmth is the
competitor's signature. We cannot borrow it.

**2. The visual already carries the shift.** The italic and the hollow grey dot
say *this is the side channel* before a word is read. When the typography
carries a distinction, the copy does not have to carry it again — doing both is
how a system ends up shouting. The words should shift **less** precisely because
the visual shifts.

**3. What changes is function, not personality.** This mode asks and narrates
where the brand voice asserts. *"Speaks in conclusions not caveats"* has no
purchase on a question — there is no conclusion to speak. So the attribute does
not soften; it takes a different form.

---

## How each attribute survives the shift

| Attribute | In the brand voice | In conversation |
|---|---|---|
| **Precise** | Every word earns its place | Unchanged. The strictest of the five here |
| **Authoritative** | Speaks in conclusions | Expressed as **specificity and closure**: a named, answerable question with real options — not an open invitation |
| **Dry wit** | Present, never announced | **Allowed in an observation, never in a request.** Wit while asking a favour is presumptuous |
| **Commercially sharp** | Anchors to numbers | Unchanged. *"reading 1,240 sources"*, never *"reading lots of sources"* |
| **Respectful** | Trusts the reader | Sharpens into **brevity**: one clause of rationale, never a paragraph |

**Authority in an interrogative** is the subtle one. These are the same question:

- ✅ *"Which sectors do you follow most?"* + three named options
- ❌ *"Tell us a bit about your areas of interest."*

The first is authoritative because it is specific, closed and answerable in one
tap. The second is a form. **Specificity is how authority survives a question.**

---

## Where the warmth comes from

Not from friendliness markers. From **situational awareness** — showing you know
what the person is doing right now.

> *"While the analysis runs — which sectors do you follow most? It sharpens
> every report."*

That sentence has no warm words in it and reads as considerate, because:

1. **It names the moment.** *"While the analysis runs"* acknowledges we are
   interrupting, and why now is acceptable.
2. **It asks one thing**, closed and answerable.
3. **It gives the reason in one clause.** *"It sharpens every report."* Not a
   sentence about how much we value their input.

That is the mechanism to reuse. Warmth by relevance, never by tone.

---

## Two grammatical permissions this mode has, and the rest of the brand does not

Neither appears in marketing copy or a report. Both are what make this read as
conversation rather than as UI text.

**1. First person singular.** Caspr says *"I"* here — *"Three ways I'd show
this"*, *"while I check today's sources"*. On the site and in a report it never
does.

**2. Contractions.** *"I'd"*, *"doesn't"*, *"let's"*. The brand voice trends
formal; this register admits them.

---

## Sentence form, by function

The brand voice permits fragments — *"15 minutes. 100 pages. Cited to source."*
Here it depends on what the sentence is doing:

- **Asking → complete sentences.** A fragment in a request reads as curt.
  *"Sectors followed?"* is a database field, not a question from a colleague.
- **Narrating status → fragments are correct.** *"sourcing stage · reading 1,240
  sources"* is right exactly as it is.

Both live in the same italic channel; the rule follows the function, not the
styling.

---

## What does NOT change

Non-negotiable, and this list is the difference between a register shift and a
personality transplant:

- **No exclamation points.** Ever. Unchanged.
- **No greetings or pleasantries.** No *"Hi"*, no *"Hope your week is going
  well"*, no *"Thanks!"*.
- **No apologising for asking.** *"Sorry to interrupt"*, *"if you have a
  moment"*, *"mind if I ask"* — all out. Ask, or do not.
- **No over-explaining the value.** One clause. Never a sentence about how the
  answer helps us help you.
- **No emoji.**
- **No "let me know if you have any questions."**
- **No hedging the ask.** *"You might want to consider telling us…"* is not a
  question.
- **Never claims what the engine will do.** The standing boundary: this channel
  speaks about the user and the product, never on Caspr's behalf about the
  analysis.

## Calibration — approved

- *"While the analysis runs — which sectors do you follow most? It sharpens
  every report."*
- *"Three ways I'd show this — composition, trend, or share-shift. Pick one, or
  tell me."*
- *"sourcing stage · reading 1,240 sources"*
- *"Who reads this one — you, or a committee?"*
- *"Noted. That sharpens the next one."*
- *"You have run four studies this month. Worth knowing: Intelligence goes
  deeper on the same question."*

## Calibration — not approved

| What was written | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|
| *"Hi Joy! While we work on that, mind if I ask a quick question?"* | Greeting, exclamation, apology for asking. Three failures in one line |
| *"Sectors followed?"* | A form field. Curt where the brand is precise |
| *"Tell us a bit about the kind of research you do — it really helps us tailor things for you!"* | Open-ended, padded, exclamation. Reads as a survey |
| *"Great choice! I'll make sure to weight that in the analysis."* | Praise, exclamation, and a promise about the engine the product cannot make |
| *"I'm so glad you asked."* | Chatbot. The single fastest way to lose the category claim |
| *"Just checking in while that runs…"* | *"Just"* hedges, and nothing is being asked |

---

## The two tests

Every line in this channel passes both, or it does not ship.

1. **The chatbot test.** *Would this sentence be at home in a generative
   assistant?* If yes, cut it. This defends the positioning.
2. **The consultant test.** *Would a good consultant say this, to this person,
   at this moment?* If no, say nothing. This defends the user's time.

The second permits silence, and silence is a legitimate output. The first is the
one people will fail, because warmth is the default instinct in product copy and
here it is the risk.

---

## Consequence for the acknowledgement

Earlier candidate was *"Filed: runs diligence on live deals."* In this register
that is a shade too systemy — accurate, but it sounds like a database confirming
a write.

**Proposed: *"Noted. That sharpens the next one."***

It echoes the question's own rationale (*"It sharpens every report"*), claims
nothing about the running analysis, and is warm by relevance rather than by
tone. Two short sentences, no promise, no praise.
