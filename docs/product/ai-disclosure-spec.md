# AI disclosure — EU AI Act Article 50

**Status:** design locked 2026-08-21. Answers §7 of `../app-handoff/DESIGN-INPUTS-WHILE-CONVENTIONS-SETTLE.md`.
**In force since 2 August 2026**, regardless of risk tier.

Two obligations, two very different surfaces. One is nearly invisible; the other is real engineering.

---

## 1 · Article 50(1) — disclose that the user is interacting with an AI system

*"…unless this is obvious from the point of view of a natural person who is reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect."*

**Caspr sits comfortably inside that exemption and we should still not rely on it alone.** The category is stated as *Analytical AI* on the marketing site, the homepage H1 is *"Not an assistant. An analyst."*, and the product's entire pitch is machine-generated analysis. A user cannot plausibly believe a human wrote a 100-page report in 15 minutes for $80.

**So: no banner, no modal, no interstitial.** Any of those would be a compliance gesture that damages the first-run experience — the exact screen where activation is already failing.

**What we do instead — a persistent, quiet, permanent link.**

- **Added to every onboarding footer** (19 frames, desktop and mobile): `Security · Privacy · Terms · AI disclosure · © 2026 Caspr`
- Links to a short `/ai-disclosure` page stating: that Caspr is an AI system, what the Thinking Brain and Learning Brain do, that outputs are generated and cited, and where sources come from.
- The desktop footer is now **right-anchored to x=1320** and the mobile footer centred — the added item pushed the old fixed position past the 120px margin.

**Deliberately not done:** a first-run banner. If counsel later requires an explicit in-session notice, the cheapest compliant place is one line beneath the prompt bar in `text/tertiary`, not a dismissible bar. **That is a legal call, not a design one** — flagged, not decided.

## 2 · Article 50(2) — AI-generated content must be marked, machine-readably

**This is the obligation with teeth, and Caspr's entire deliverable is generated content.** It applies to every PDF and PPTX we produce.

### 2.1 Visible mark — on the deliverable

On the **report cover**, in the cover foot, below the title block:

> **Generated by Caspr · Analytical AI · {date}**

Set in the cover's existing foot treatment. It is a statement of provenance, in the same register as a byline — not a disclaimer, not a warning, and never apologetic. It sits beside the existing `Type · Depth` eyebrow rather than competing with it.

**Also in the PDF/PPTX document footer**, alongside the page number: `Caspr · generated {date}`.

### 2.2 Machine-readable mark — the actual requirement

A visible line does **not** satisfy 50(2). The marking must be machine-readable and the content detectable as AI-generated.

**PDF — XMP metadata:**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| `xmp:CreatorTool` | `Caspr Analytical AI` |
| `dc:creator` | `Caspr` |
| `dc:date` | ISO-8601 generation timestamp |
| `caspr:aiGenerated` | `true` |
| `caspr:generatedAt` | ISO-8601 |
| `caspr:analysisType` · `caspr:depth` | the Type and Depth (`analysis-taxonomy`) |
| `caspr:sourceCount` | integer |

**PPTX — OOXML core properties:** `dc:creator = Caspr`, `cp:lastModifiedBy = Caspr`, plus a custom property part carrying the same `caspr:*` fields.

**The durable answer is C2PA Content Credentials** — a signed provenance manifest embedded in the file, which is what the emerging detection ecosystem actually reads. The `caspr:*` fields above are the pragmatic interim. **Recommendation: ship the XMP/OOXML fields now, and put C2PA on the roadmap** rather than treating custom fields as a permanent solution.

### 2.3 What must not happen

- **The mark must survive editing.** A user editing a report in-product must not strip the metadata; regeneration must re-stamp it.
- **The mark is not conditional on tier.** Free-trial output carries it identically.
- **Do not mark uploaded source documents.** Files in the Data Room are the user's, not generated by us. Only the deliverable is marked.
