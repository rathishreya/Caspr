# The Guerrilla Campaign — operating plan

*2026-08-24. The position is settled in [`guerrilla-evaluation.md`](guerrilla-evaluation.md); this is how it runs.*

**Reads alongside:** [`launch-plan.md`](launch-plan.md) *(the arc and the spread mechanics)* · [`content-calendar.md`](content-calendar.md) *(the twelve weeks)*

---

## 1 · The one rule, restated because everything below depends on it

> **Attack the format and the economics. Never the firms or their data quality.**

Not squeamishness — arithmetic. **We cite these publishers as sources.** Discrediting them discredits our own output, because Caspr runs on borrowed authority: its credibility is transferred from the sources it names. *(Full reasoning: `guerrilla-evaluation.md` §2.1.)*

**Every piece below is therefore built from facts about the model** — price, date, coverage, specificity — **and never from judgements about the work.** *"Published fourteen months ago"* is a fact nobody can rebut. *"Unreliable"* is an opinion we cannot afford to hold in public.

## 2 · The escalation ladder

**You do not start at maximum aggression. You earn the right to escalate**, and each rung is gated on the previous one having run without blowing up.

| Rung | What it does | Who is named | Risk | Gate to reach it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Publish analysis** — the industry, analysed | **Nobody** | None | — |
| **2** | **The staleness index** — publisher and date, stated | Publishers, **factually** | Low | Rung 1 published, no factual challenge upheld |
| **3** | **Correct the record** — trace one canonical figure | A figure, and its chain | Medium | Rung 2 running two months clean |
| **4** | **Price transparency** — what research actually costs | The market, not a firm | Medium-high | OD-10 closed · counsel cleared |
| **5** | **The open challenge** — compare us to what you paid for | Us | **Highest, and it is ours** | Issues 1–3 have established quality |

**Rung 1 is the only one that runs before the week-four funnel gate.** Everything above it is part of the launch campaign and inherits its go/no-go.

**Nothing skips a rung.** If rung 2 draws a correction we have to publish, rung 3 waits a month.

## 3 · Rung 1 — *The Record*, issue 1: the research industry, analysed

**Published week 3. Joy commissions it, end of week 1.**

### What is in it

A real Caspr Study on the market research and business information industry:

| | Section |
|---|---|
| **1** | Market size and growth, split by segment — syndicated, custom, panel and fieldwork, analytics |
| **2** | Concentration: who owns what, and how much of the market the largest groups hold |
| **3** | **Pricing structure** — what a syndicated report costs, what a custom engagement costs, and how the range is distributed |
| **4** | Margin structure, where it is publicly visible |
| **5** | Where revenue is shifting, and what is growing against what is not |
| **6** | **Publication cadence** — how often the major products refresh, stated as a fact |

**Sections 3 and 6 carry the whole argument, and neither of them makes a claim.** They state what things cost and how often they update. The reader draws the conclusion.

### The one line that does the work

**Stated once, dryly, in the executive summary — and never repeated anywhere:**

> This analysis of the research industry is an example of what the research industry sells. It took two hours and cost eighty dollars.

**Do not put it in the headline, the social posts, or the press release.** It is a closing observation, not a boast. Repeated, it becomes the gloating this campaign exists to avoid.

### Guardrails

- **No named firm is criticised.** Named as market participants and as sources, never as failures
- **Public data only.** No leaked pricing, no confidential documents
- **The disclosure note** in the cover foot — `guerrilla-evaluation.md` §5.1
- **Every figure sourced**, including the ones about firms we compete with. Especially those

### Production

| | |
|---|---|
| **End week 1** | Joy commissions. Topic is not from the demand pipeline — there is no prompt data yet, and that is stated rather than disguised |
| **Weeks 1–2** | Run, then **reviewed harder than anything else this quarter.** It sets the publication's register permanently |
| **Week 3** | Publish. Report page, PDF, the atom, the derivatives |
| **Week 3+** | Response cycle — Joy answers substantive replies personally |

## 4 · Rung 2 — the staleness index

**Launches at the campaign peak, weeks 10–12, as a standing monthly feature.**

### What it contains, per sector

| Column | Example of the content |
|---|---|
| Sector | GICS sector and sub-industry |
| **The most-cited figure** | The market size that appears most often in circulation |
| **Original publisher** | Who actually measured it |
| **Publication date** | When |
| **Age** | In months, at time of publication |
| **Repetitions** | **How many other publications carry the same number without new measurement** |

**The last column is the devastating one and it is also the hardest to produce.** It means searching for the figure and counting where it recurs. **Budget real hours for it, or cut it to a spot-check on three sectors rather than claiming a full count.** A miscounted column is a correction we would have to publish.

### The check that must not be skipped

> **Before publishing that a figure is the latest, check the publisher's own site.**

**A publisher who issued an update we missed is the single most likely error in this campaign**, and it is the one that would hand them a free rebuttal. One person, one pass, every issue.

### Guardrails

- **State facts. Never state judgement.** Publisher, date, age. Never *"outdated"*, *"stale in a bad way"*, *"unreliable"*
- **The word "staleness" belongs to the index's internal name.** In published copy, the column is *Age*, and the piece is *how old the numbers are*
- **Where a figure is genuinely current, say so.** An index that never has good news is an argument, not a measurement — and it will be read as one

### Where it lives

**Not only a monthly post.** Every `/market-size/[sector]-[geography]` page carries the age of the incumbent figure for that sector — so the index is a **permanent, per-sector, linkable surface** that the monthly issue summarises. That is also what makes it shareable per sector rather than per issue.

## 5 · Rung 3 — correct the record

**One canonical figure per issue, traced to its origin.**

### The work

1. Identify a number in wide circulation in a target sector
2. **Trace it backwards** — who repeated it, who they cited, and where the chain terminates
3. Establish what the original measurement actually was: its base year, its definition, its method
4. Publish the chain, and the current figure with its own sourcing

**This is real journalism and it is the most labour-intensive item in the whole plan.** It is not generatable. **It needs a named owner and roughly a day per issue** — and if that owner does not exist, this rung does not run. Say so rather than scheduling it and quietly dropping it.

### The register

**We are not exposing a fraud. We are showing a chain.** Most stale canonical figures exist because everyone repeated a reasonable number and nobody re-measured — that is a systemic property, not a scandal, and describing it as a scandal would be both wrong and off-brand.

**Never:** *"X got it wrong."* **Always:** *"This figure originates in a 2023 measurement of a differently-defined market. Here is the chain, and here is what it is now."*

## 6 · Rung 4 — price transparency

**~~Gated on OD-10~~ — OD-10 closed 2026-08-25. Still gated on counsel.**

**Its sourced basis is now stronger than when this rung was written.** [`od-10-results.md`](../od-10-results.md) §3 establishes that **price opacity is the industry norm** — Mintel, Euromonitor, Gartner, Forrester, PitchBook and Grand View all quote-only — and §1A.1 now shows, across **five reports and two publishers**, that where prices *are* published they **do not move with pages, sector or year.** Both are facts about the model, stated without an adjective, which is exactly what §4's guardrail requires.

### Permitted sources

| ✅ Use | ⛔ Never |
|---|---|
| Published rate cards and price lists | **A quote obtained by posing as a buyer** |
| Public tender and procurement records | Any quote given under confidentiality |
| Prices customers volunteer, with permission | Leaked internal documents |
| Prices stated in public filings or press | Anything a person shares that identifies them without consent |

> **Do not gather quotes covertly.** Requesting a proposal without intending to buy, in order to publish the price, is a misrepresentation — and for a company selling defensibility it is the exact wrong risk to take for the exact wrong reason. **Public records and volunteered figures only.**

**And the standing rule holds: do not invent a competitor's price.** Inventing one on a page whose entire argument is *"we cite"* is the worst own goal available in this plan.

## 7 · Rung 5 — the open challenge

> *"Send us the question you paid for. We will answer it and publish ours."*

### How it actually works, given copyright

**We cannot republish their report.** So:

1. The participant sends **the question**, not the document
2. We run it, publish our answer in full, and say what it cost and how long it took
3. **They** compare, and comment publicly if they choose. We never characterise their report on their behalf

### The commitment that makes it credible

> **We publish the result either way.**

**A challenge you could never lose is not a challenge**, and an audience of analysts will see that instantly. Committing in advance to publish a loss is what makes the win worth anything — and a loss published honestly is worth more than a win published triumphantly. It is format 4.4, *what we got wrong*, working as designed.

## 8 · The pre-publication checklist — every piece, every time

| | Check |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Every figure carries a resolvable source.** No exceptions, including figures about competitors |
| **2** | **No firm is described as inaccurate, unreliable or low quality.** Search the draft for evaluative adjectives about a named company |
| **3** | **Currency check** — for any claim about a publication's age, the publisher's own site has been checked this week |
| **4** | **No competitor named in a headline, a hero or an ad.** `CLAUDE.md` rule 1 |
| **5** | **No reader labour** — no *verify*, *check the working*, *see for yourself*. Rule 2 |
| **6** | **The disclosure note** is present on anything touching the research sector |
| **7** | **Text hygiene pass** — `CLAUDE.md` Rule 6, before it leaves |
| **8** | **Counsel** has cleared the first piece at each new rung. Not every piece — the first at each rung |

## 9 · When they respond

| They | We |
|---|---|
| **Publicly rebut** | **The best outcome available.** Engage substantively, publish their argument in full, answer it. Argument is distribution, and a serious opponent confers standing we cannot buy |
| **Correct us** | **Publish the correction, credit them by name, thank them plainly.** It costs nothing and it proves the standard. Then fix the process that let it through |
| **Send a legal letter** | Counsel first. **Do not respond publicly, and do not publish the letter.** Oatly made that work; their brand is provocation and ours is not. Quietly amend anything unsupportable |
| **Say nothing** | **Expected. Keep publishing, and never taunt.** *"They haven't responded"* is the sentence that turns an analyst into a heckler |

**The register is the analyst, not the challenger. We never gloat and we never punch down** — and if a piece would read differently coming from a company ten times our size, it is the wrong piece.

## 11 · Measurement

**Per `launch-plan.md` §5 — impressions do not count.**

| Signal | What it tells us |
|---|---|
| **Public argument with a conclusion** | It was read and taken seriously. The primary signal |
| **Corrections and contributions received** | The contribution mechanic is live |
| **Citations by practitioners or trade press** | It entered the discourse |
| **Coverage requests** — *"do this for my sector"* | Demand, volunteered, and a direct pipeline input |
| **Subscriptions** | The audience that survives a weak `x` |

**And the diagnostic that stops us fooling ourselves:** an absence of corrections is only evidence of rigour **if the other signals are present.** If nothing at all comes back, we are not unchallenged — we are unread, and the fix is distribution, not a claim about quality.
