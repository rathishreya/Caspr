# The Guerrilla Option — an evaluation

*2026-08-24. Should Caspr run a loud disruptor campaign against the incumbent research firms?*

**Short answer: the aggression is right, the target is wrong.** There is a real attack surface and we should use it hard — but attacking the firms or their data would discredit our own product, and the reason is structural rather than squeamish.

---

## 1 · The instinct is right. The advantage is real

**This would not be a bluff.** Three claims are defensible today:

| | Caspr | A syndicated report |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost** | $80 | $5,000–15,000; a consultancy engagement starts above $50,000 |
| **Time** | 15 minutes to 2 hours | 2–6 weeks, or a fixed publication cycle |
| **Recency** | Regenerated on live feeds | **A snapshot, published once, ageing from day one** |
| **Specificity** | The exact intersection asked for | *"Food delivery, globally"* when the question was premium meal kits in Southeast England |

**Recency and specificity are the sharp ones**, and they are structural rather than competitive — no incumbent can fix them without abandoning the syndicated model that pays for them. `icp-personas.md:536` is a buyer describing precisely this: *"Syndicated data doesn't always cover my specific subcategory. When it doesn't, I build it manually."*

**So there is something real to attack.** The question is what.

---

## 2 · Three reasons the obvious version is fatal. One is decisive

### 2.1 ⛔ We cite the people we would be attacking

**This is the one that ends the argument.**

Caspr's output is a synthesis of published research. `fact_lookup` returns figures attributed to industry bodies, government databases and syndicated publishers — and the whole product promise is *every source, credible.*

> **We cannot argue that syndicated research is unreliable and then cite syndicated research as proof that our answer is trustworthy.**

Run a campaign asserting Euromonitor's data is stale, and every Caspr report citing Euromonitor is now citing a source we publicly called stale. **We would have spent money attacking our own inputs.**

The psychology has a name: Caspr runs on **borrowed authority.** Its credibility is transferred from the sources it names — that is the entire mechanism by which a $80 report survives a boardroom. **Discrediting the source discredits the transfer.**

### 2.2 The buyer already believes the incumbent is credible — and often pays for it

Attacking IBISWorld to someone whose firm subscribes to IBISWorld tells them their judgement was poor. *Confirmation bias runs the other way here: fighting an existing belief head-on rarely works — it hardens it.*

**And this buyer is the wrong one for a fight.** The leading objection for our primary ICP is *"our compliance team reviews all third-party research tools"* (`:276`). **A loud, combative vendor is a harder compliance sell, not an easier one.** A PE associate putting Caspr output in an IC memo has their own credibility at stake; they are not shopping for a brawl.

### 2.3 It would cost us the testimonials, and a future segment

The ten testimonials in production come from **senior market research practitioners.** A campaign attacking the research industry asks those people to endorse a company attacking their profession. **Some will decline, and they would be right to.**

It also burns ICP 6 permanently — a $250-MRR segment whose objection table already contains *"Isn't this a threat to my job?"* (`:557`). Deprioritised is not the same as destroyed.

## 3 · The case studies — and the pattern in them

| Campaign | What it actually attacked | Transfers? |
|---|---|---|
| **Pepsi Challenge** | Nothing. It ran a **demonstration** and let the audience judge | **Strongly.** This is the model |
| **HubSpot — "inbound"** | A **practice** (interruption marketing), never a company | **Yes.** Attack the practice |
| **Salesforce — "No Software"** | A **delivery model** (on-premise), not vendor competence. The Siebel-conference stunt was theatre around a real category claim | **Partly.** B2B and it worked — but Benioff had fame and funding we do not |
| **Avis — "We're number two"** | Nothing. It named its own position honestly | **Yes.** Underdog candour, no aggression needed |
| **Oatly vs. the dairy lobby** | An **industry**, by name. Got sued, lost, published the lawsuit, grew | **No.** Consumer mass market, no compliance gate, and no shared supply chain |
| **Dollar Shave Club** | A competitor's **pricing**, with humour | **No.** Consumer, low stakes — and announced humour is off-register for us |
| **Ryanair** | Competitors, abrasively | **No.** Price-led consumer category |

> **The pattern is consistent.** The campaigns that transferred attacked a **model, a practice, or a price structure**, and demonstrated an alternative. The ones that attacked a **company's competence** worked only in consumer categories with low stakes, no gatekeepers, and no shared inputs.

**Caspr has high stakes, a compliance gatekeeper, and shared inputs.** All three point the same way.

---

## 4 · The reframe — and it is sharper, not softer

> **Attack the format and the economics. Never the firms or their data quality.**

The distinction in one line:

- ❌ *"Euromonitor's numbers are unreliable."* — attacks our own input, insults the buyer, invites a lawyer
- ✅ *"That report is excellent, it cost $12,000, it was published fourteen months ago, and it does not answer your question."*

**The second is unarguable.** No incumbent can rebut *"published fourteen months ago"* — it is a fact about the model, not a slur about the work. **And it leaves every source intact to cite.**

It also lets us keep the incumbent's authority working *for* us: the report is good, and being good is not the same as being current, specific, or affordable.

## 5 · Four campaigns that are genuinely aggressive and survive the analysis

### 5.1 Run Caspr on the market research industry itself — **THE RECORD, ISSUE 1. Approved 2026-08-24**

**Publish a full Caspr analysis of the research industry** — its size, growth, margin structure, pricing, concentration, and where the revenue actually comes from.

**Why this is the boldest thing on the list.** It turns the instrument on the incumbents without saying a word against them. It is dry, it concludes, and it demonstrates the product on the one topic this audience knows well enough to judge — *"test it on a sector you know well"* (`:279`), executed as a campaign.

**Risk: low.** It is analysis. **Impact: high** — it is the kind of artefact that circulates in exactly the right rooms, and every number in it is cited.

**It publishes in week 3, not at the launch peak, and that is deliberate.** The build-up's job is to make the launch land with proof rather than a promise, and this is the proof. Published early it gets nine weeks to circulate, be argued with and be cited before the announcement; published at the peak it gets a spike and no history. **It also lands a week before the week-four funnel gate — so the engagement it generates is evidence going into that decision rather than a bet resting on it.**

#### The disclosure — decided 2026-08-24

Issue 1 analyses an industry Caspr's founders also operate in. **Standing note on the issue, in the cover foot:**

> **Disclosure.** Caspr's founders hold operating interests in the sector analysed here. Every figure below is attributed to its source.

**Two lines, no apology, and it ends by restating the standard rather than hedging the work.** The firm is not named — the Ghost boundary holds, and naming it is not what a disclosure is for. What matters is that the reader is told there is an interest before they read, not after someone else points it out.

**And it strengthens the piece rather than weakening it.** An undisclosed interest, surfaced later, is a story about us. A disclosed one reads as an insider who showed their working — which is the position.

**Applies to every issue touching the research sector**, not only issue 1.

---

### 5.2 The staleness index — the recurring one

**Publish, by sector, how old the most-cited market figure actually is.** Monthly. The canonical number for a category, its publisher, its publication date, and how many other sources have simply repeated it since.

**This is the whole attack, made factual and repeatable.** It is checkable, un-rebuttable, and it converts our one genuinely structural advantage into a recurring news event. **It is also the perfect standing feature for The Record** — and it feeds the *correct the record* flagship format directly.

### 5.3 The open challenge — highest risk, and the risk is on us

> *"Send us the report you paid $15,000 for. We will answer the same question and publish ours."*

**A demonstration, not a claim — the Pepsi Challenge structure.** The risk sits entirely on our side of the table, which is what makes it credible rather than boastful.

**One hard constraint:** we cannot republish their copyrighted report. The comparison is our output against their *published question*, or run by the customer and reported by them.

### 5.4 Price transparency — attack a practice, not a firm

The research industry does not publish its prices. *"Contact us for a quote"* is the norm.

**Publishing what research actually costs — sourced, with real quotes — attacks an industry practice rather than any company.** It is the HubSpot move, and it is unarguable because it is our own procurement experience.

**~~Gated on OD-10~~ — closed 2026-08-25** ([`../od-10-results.md`](../od-10-results.md)). **The prohibition it existed to enforce does not lift with it:** every figure is a published list price read from the seller's own page with an observation date, and **a vendor's blog range is never a market fact** (§4 there). Inventing a competitor's price on a page whose argument is *"we cite"* would still be the single worst own goal available.

## 6 · What we will not do, and why

| | Ruled out |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Naming a firm as unreliable, inaccurate or low quality.** §2.1 — it discredits our own citations |
| **2** | **Stunts.** Caspr's wit is *"never announced, never explained."* **A stunt is announced humour by construction** — off-register, and it reads as unserious to a buyer whose main worry is compliance |
| **3** | **Attacking the market research profession or its practitioners.** §2.3 — it costs us the testimonials and burns ICP 6 |
| **4** | **Anything in lead copy.** `CLAUDE.md` rule 1 stands: no competitor named in a hero, a headline or an ad. This lives in The Record, on `/alternatives/*`, in social and in founder writing |

## 7 · Risk register

| | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Legal** | Comparative advertising is lawful in the US, UK and EU when claims are **truthful, verifiable, and compare like with like** — but EU rules additionally prohibit **discrediting** a competitor's mark. Caspr is Singapore-incorporated selling into the EU | **Every comparative claim carries its source and its date. Counsel reviews the first one.** This is a legal question, flagged not answered |
| **Reputational** | The loud disruptor is scrutinised harder than the quiet vendor. Our own output becomes the story | **Only run this after the funnel gate.** Aggression before the product's first hour is polished invites exactly the wrong audit |
| **Relational** | Testimonial practitioners, and Ghost's ongoing work | The reframe in §4 removes this entirely. The firms are never the target |
| **Strategic** | A fight makes us look like a peer of the incumbents — or like a small company shouting at large ones | **Avis's lesson: candour about position beats aggression.** Demonstration outranks argument every time |

---

## 8 · Recommendation

**Take the bold bet, in bounded form** — 80% of effort on the proven plan, a contained share on the high-variance play, and nothing in the mediocre middle.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Now** | Nothing. This runs after the week-four funnel gate, alongside the launch campaign |
| **Week 3** | **§5.1 — Caspr on the research industry. THE RECORD, ISSUE 1.** Lands in the build-up, not at the peak: nine weeks to circulate, and a week before the funnel gate it informs |
| **Launch peak** | **§5.2 — the staleness index launches** as a standing monthly feature. A one-off issue gives peak attention something to read; **a recurring index gives it a reason to subscribe** |
| **Once proof exists** | **§5.3 — the open challenge**, and **§5.4 — price transparency** — **OD-10 closed 2026-08-25; counsel is the remaining gate** |

**What we are actually buying with this.** Not a fight. **A position: the analyst who publishes what everyone else sells, current, at a hundredth of the price.** That is a more aggressive claim than any insult, and the incumbents cannot answer it — because answering it would mean abandoning the model that funds them.
