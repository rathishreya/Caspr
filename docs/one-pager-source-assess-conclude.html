Brand architecture · Internal · 27 Aug 2026

Two brains retired. Three stages in their place.

The Thinking Brain and the Learning Brain came from a pitch deck written over a year ago. Both are now retired — together, and permanently. What replaces them is not a rename. It is the proof line and the architecture collapsed into one asset.

“Thinking” stopped being ours.

It is now a loading state on every frontier model. A buyer who reads Thinking Brain hears we do what every chatbot does — which is the one sentence our entire position exists to refuse.

“Learning” was never accurate.

Nothing learns. The word described live data feeds, and it invited exactly the question we cannot answer well: learning from whose data? A term that is both wrong and legally awkward is not worth defending.

The architecture is the proof line

Source. Assess. Conclude.

  • SourceEvery source, credible. The 25M+ curated corpus, the live feeds that keep it current, and your own material.
  • AssessEvery claim, triangulated. Reconciling what the sources disagree on — and separating a definitional difference from a genuine one.
  • ConcludeEvery report, defensible. A position that survives the room, not a summary.

The units nest: source → claim → report. Each is larger than the last, and the guarantee escalates with it.

The promise and the mechanism used to be two things a reader had to remember. Now they are one. “Every claim, triangulated” is also countable — our reports carry 355–678 cited claims each.

Never find-and-replace this.

Two brains do not map onto three stages. The Learning Brain was not Source, and the Thinking Brain was not Assess — it did the concluding too. Anywhere the old terms appear, the sentence has to be rewritten, not substituted.

Three words that are now forbidden.

Brain, thinking and learning — anywhere near the architecture. Not in decks, copy, the app, report covers, or a conversation with a customer. Reports now read: “Sourced, assessed and concluded by Caspr.”
