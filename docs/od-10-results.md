## 4 · A distinction that must not be blurred

**Two kinds of number surfaced during this research, and only one is citable in the sense Caspr means:**

| | |
|---|---|
| ✅ **A published list price** | Read from the seller's own page, with a URL and an observation date. Everything in §1 |
| ⚠ **A vendor-stated range in a blog post** | *"$20,000–$50,000 for a custom project"*, *"$5,000–15,000 for 10–15 interviews"* — from agency marketing content ([Drive Research](https://www.driveresearch.com/market-research-company-blog/how-much-does-market-research-cost/), [The Farnsworth Group](https://www.thefarnsworthgroup.com/blog/market-research-cost)) |

**The second kind is useful and it is not the same thing.** It is a seller describing their own market, in content written to win that market's business — **the *"supplier-funded research is biased"* problem at `icp-personas.md:631`, applied to pricing.**

**Where a range is used, attribute it as a claim by a named firm, never as a market fact.** *"Drive Research states $20,000–50,000"* is defensible. *"Custom research costs $20,000–50,000"* is not.
