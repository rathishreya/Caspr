## Voice — the hard constraints

**The Trusted Senior Analyst.** The most credible person in the room. Arrives with the numbers done. Speaks in
conclusions, not descriptions. Never wastes a word.

- **Precise** — every word earns its place
- **Authoritative** — conclusions, not hedges
- **Dry wit** — intelligence is the humour; never announced
- **Commercially sharp** — numbers anchor everything. "15 minutes", not "fast"
- **Respectful** — trusts the reader; never over-explains

**Never use:** exclamation points (ever) · platform · leverages · algorithms · workflows · powerful AI ·
revolutionary · game-changing · chatbot · web scraping · hallucinate · "excited to announce" · "Here's how:"

**Calibration — this is the target:**
> "15 minutes. 100 pages. Cited to source."
> "The $200,000 question. For $80."
> "Analyst-grade insights. Without the analyst retainer."
> "While the world was building generative AI, we built analytical AI."

## Method — write in layers, not page by page

**This is the single most important instruction.** Voice drift across 40 pages is the main risk, and writing
page-by-page guarantees it. Instead:

1. **All eyebrows, site-wide.** One pass. They should read as one set.
2. **All headlines, site-wide.** One pass. Read them as a list before moving on — any that sound like a
   different writer, rewrite.
3. **All body copy**, fold by fold.
4. **All CTAs and microcopy.**
5. **Read the headline list once more** against the calibration examples above.

## Positioning — the thing most likely to go wrong

Caspr competes with **research**, not with AI tools. Write so the reader's comparison is *"what this costs from a
research firm"*, never *"what ChatGPT gives me for $20."* The LLM comparison is argued **only** on `/vs/chatgpt`
and `/vs/perplexity`. Raising it anywhere else invites the frame we are trying to displace.

Order the argument: **their question vs yours** first · **today's data** second · **15 minutes** third ·
**cost** fourth. Leading with cheapness invites the LLM frame.

## Blocked — do not invent

The **comparison folds** on every ICP and use-case page, and the homepage "What research costs" band, depend on
real cited comparables being sourced (`docs/od-10-research-brief.md`, tracked as OD-10). **Write the fold's
surrounding copy and leave the figures as clearly-marked placeholders.** Do not invent competitor prices,
publication dates or page counts — the brand's entire claim is that it cites.

Testimonials and the TRUSTED BY logo row are placeholders pending real ones. Leave as-is; do not write new
fictional attributed quotes.
