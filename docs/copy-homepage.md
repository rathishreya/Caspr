## 4 · The comparison — NEW · the fold that does the most work

**Eyebrow:** ANALYTICAL AI
**Headline:** Buy the report. Commission the study. Or ask the question.

**Definition line — the AEO block, and the entity sentence:**
> **Caspr is Analytical AI — purpose-built for business analysis rather than conversation.** You ask a question
> in one line. It proposes a structure, reads 25 million curated sources, weighs what they disagree on, and
> returns a cited analysis — a quick read in fifteen minutes, a full one in a couple of hours.

### The four buckets

**It answers your question.**
A published report answers the question its publisher chose. One European ready-meals report runs to ten countries at $4,750 — and stops at national level. The question was Southeast England.
Caspr goes to the intersection you asked about, and reads your own files alongside its sources.

**You can see the working.**
Between 355 and 678 cited claims in a typical analysis, every one of them attributed. 87% of dated references are from 2025 or 2026.
Before you buy a syndicated report, there is no way to establish whether any single claim inside it is sourced.

**An analyst, not a document.**
Refine a section. Re-visualise a chart. Any claim will tell you where it came from. It writes in your register — and from the Business plan, in your own template and your own language. *Updates, so an analysis moves when the market does — coming soon.*

**Yours to use.**
No licence tier. The same syndicated report is $4,490 for one reader and $8,490 for a team. No call, no procurement conversation — and six of nine major publishers name no price publicly at all.

**The line under the buckets:** *We don't charge by the page.*

**Footnote:** Data upload, all output formats and all languages from the Business plan.
**CTA:** `Run the analysis` · See a sample report → · **Where we don't win** → `/alternatives`

**Source:** `report-evaluation-2026-08` §10.1 *(355–678 cited claims; 87% 2025–26; measured across 23 analyses,
August 2026)* · §10.2 *(the pre-purchase sourcing gap — "the only dimension where the gap is a difference in
kind")* · §9.1 *(ready meals, ten countries, national floor)* · `od-10-results` §1.2 *($4,490 → $8,490 licence
ladder, observed 2026-08-24)* · §3 *(six of nine publish no price)* · `icp-personas:536`.

**Note — four things this fold is doing deliberately.**
**No competitor is named** (rule 1.1) — the reports are described, not the firms, and every figure carries a
citable origin in the deck's source line. **The category is established here** rather than in a headline, and
the contrast is with *published research*, not with the LLM category — which keeps rule 1.1 intact while still
saying what Analytical AI means. **Cost sits in bucket four**, last (rule 1.3). And the two measured numbers
need a visible *measured August 2026* stamp: they age, and unlike the incumbent's publication date they do not
improve on their own.

**⚠ Flag:** *"Weighs what they disagree on"* in the definition line is a **level-two-and-above property**
(rule 2.5). It is true of a Study and not yet of a Brief. Either the line is qualified, or the Brief template
change in `report-style-guide` §9b lands before this ships.

## 7 · Trust — NEW

**Eyebrow:** TRUST
**Headline:** Every claim, traceable. Every upload, yours.

**Ask Caspr.** Every claim in every analysis carries its source. The reasoning and the supporting data sit behind it, inline, in the report itself. → `/ask-caspr`
**Your data.** Uploaded files are processed inside Caspr's own infrastructure and never reach an external AI provider. They never train any model, ours or anyone's. ISO 27001:2022, independently audited. → `/security`

**Source:** `gtm-strategy` §12 — the three-part answer to the #1 conversion barrier, which §12 says belongs on
the homepage objection section. `security-posture` §1–2.

**Note:** The homepage had **no objection fold at all**, which is a missing CRO dimension rather than a missing
feature. These are the two blocking objections and they are the same emotion — *can I trust the output* and
*can I trust you with my work*. Deliberately phrased without instructing the reader (rule 1.2): the reasoning
*sits behind* the claim; the reader is not told to go and check it.

## 9 · Frequently asked — NEW

**Eyebrow:** FREQUENTLY ASKED

**What is Caspr?**
Analytical AI, purpose-built for business analysis. You ask one question; it proposes a structure, reads 25M+ curated sources, and returns a cited analysis in about fifteen minutes — as a document, not a conversation. → `/about`

**Is AI-generated research credible enough to present?**
Every claim carries its source, and between 355 and 678 of them appear in a typical analysis. What you present is the underlying source, attributed — the same standard as citing any research database. → `/trust/citing-sources`

**Can I use it in a client deliverable or an IC memo?**
Yes. You cite the underlying sources rather than Caspr, exactly as you would with a terminal pull or a subscription database. → `/trust/client-deliverables`

**What does an analysis cost?**
Analyses run from $15 to $300 depending on depth, against a monthly Research Budget you set. Unused balance carries forward. → `/pricing`

**Source:** Questions taken from `presence-metric.md` §4 baskets — `C8` is *"can I cite AI-generated research
in a formal memo"* verbatim. Not invented (rule 6.5).

**Note:** This is the highest-authority page on the domain and therefore the best citation surface we own —
`FAQPage` schema here is for **answer engines only**; Google deprecated FAQ rich results on 7 May 2026, so it
must never be justified on those grounds. Cost appears here as a range rather than in a fold, which keeps
rule 1.3 intact while answering a question a buyer genuinely has before clicking.
