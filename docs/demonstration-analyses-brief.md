## 2 · ⚠ Commission once, for three jobs

**The trap: commissioning three reports for `/samples` alone.** They would be good samples and **they would not
serve the comparison folds**, because a shadow report has to answer *the same question a priced incumbent
claims to answer.* That framing has to be in the commission, not added afterwards.

> **Same three analyses. One commission. Three jobs.**

| Job | What it needs from the analysis |
|---|---|
| **Proof** — `/samples` | It stands alone as a thing a stranger reads and believes |
| **Comparison** — the folds | It shadows a named, priced incumbent report, on the question the incumbent does not answer |
| **Origination** — the content engine | It is a published Caspr analysis, which is the only thing the GTM layer may draw facts from |

---

## 3 · ⚠ The selection rule that matters most

**Pick subjects where the published sources disagree.**

**A subject where every source agrees produces a report that looks like everyone else's.** *"Every alternative,
weighed"* is a third of the proof line — and it is **invisible unless there is something to weigh.**

**We already know where the disagreements are.** Seven query shapes were tested live and every one carried an
unreconciled contradiction:

| Subject | The contradiction, observed |
|---|---|
| **UK ready meals** | **$5.86bn · 4.95% CAGR** against **$6.46bn · 12.4% CAGR** — same market, same base year |
| **Saudi industrial valves** | IMARC **$725.0M** against MarkNtel **$990M** — **36% apart** |
| **SaaS revenue multiples** | **8.5× NTM** against **3.3× TTM** — a 2.5× swing on a *definitional* difference |
| **Copper production** | *"Chile is largest"* against *"China is 48% of refined"* — mine vs refined |

> **These are pre-qualified subjects. The hard research is already done** — [`od-10-results.md`](od-10-results.md)
> §1A and the SERP tests behind [`gtm/index-engine.md`](gtm/index-engine.md) §1.

**And the definitional cases are the more interesting half.** *"The two most-cited copper figures measure
different things and neither page says which"* is sharper than *"two numbers differ"*, and it is unarguable.

### 4.1 Why each one

**1 · Investors.** The incumbent forecasts **$0.98bn (2025) → $3.05bn (2031), 21.46% CAGR.** That is the slide a
founder puts in front of a fund, and the number a fund has to sanity-check. **The market it describes is
smaller than forty-nine copies of the report that describes it** — a line that needs no adjective.

**2 · Consultants.** This is the sharpest demonstration we own, and it was asked for by name in the persona
research — `icp-personas.md:536`: *"Syndicated data doesn't always cover my specific subcategory… premium ready
meals in Southeast England… The analyst fills the gap manually every time."* **The $4,750 incumbent segments
across ten European countries and does not answer it.** The market also carries the live
$5.86bn-against-$6.46bn contradiction, so §3's rule is satisfied without looking for it.

**3 · The Brief.** Two jobs at once. **It shows the $15 entry tier doing real work** — the cheapest thing we
sell, on the page where people decide. And it is **the honest hard case**: an IBISWorld Spotlight is 10–20
pages for $800, the closest thing in the market to scope-equivalent. *(⚠ Whether IBISWorld covers this
particular industry with the $800 Spotlight or the $2,850 full report is unconfirmed — §9.3.)*

**The subject also carries a definitional gap we can already see.** IBISWorld sizes the industry at
**$414.3M**; most publishers size "the US EV charging market" in the billions, because they count different
things. **Nobody says which** — §9.3.

> **Run number 3 first, not last.** It is the only one that could genuinely go against us, **and a comparison
> programme that runs only its flattering cases is precisely what OD-10 was written to prevent.** If a $15
> Brief does not stand next to a 10–20 page incumbent, we need to know that before a fold ships — not after.

## 5 · What each one must contain to work as a public sample

**These are not quality preferences. Each is a way the sample could actively damage us.**

| | Requirement | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Every citation resolves.** A dead link, a paywalled source, a figure without a date | **This is the entire claim.** A broken citation on the sample page is worse than no sample page |
| **2** | **The disagreement is visible, not resolved away.** Where sources differ, both appear | §3. Averaging them is what the competition does |
| **3** | **Definitional differences are labelled as definitional** | Reporting *mine vs refined* as "sources disagree" is the exact sloppiness we sell against — [`gtm/index-engine.md`](gtm/index-engine.md) §2 |
| **4** | **No client data, no NDA material, no confidential source.** Public and licensed sources only | Obvious, and worth writing down |
| **5** | **It survives a competitor reading it closely.** Assume Mordor and IBISWorld will | They are named in the comparison |
| **6** | **Nothing older than 18 months without a re-look-up** | A stale figure on a page arguing *"we cite"* is worse than no page |
| **7** | **⚠ The AI-provenance mark is present and intact** — cover foot and XMP/OOXML, per [`product/ai-disclosure-spec.md`](product/ai-disclosure-spec.md) | **EU AI Act Article 50(2).** And per CLAUDE.md Rule 6, **the text-hygiene pass must never strip it** — we mark what we sell and clean what we publish about ourselves |

## 6 · What "shadowing" means, precisely

**For each analysis, record in Tab B of [`od-10-capture.xlsx`](od-10-capture.xlsx):** the incumbent, its price,
pages, publication date, its coverage statement copied from its own product page — and **the question Caspr
answered that it does not.**

**All of that is on the seller's free page. No purchase is required** — the ops brief covers why.

> **⛔ Never claim scope equivalence.** A 250-page global market report is **not** the same artefact as an $80
> Study, and saying otherwise is the overclaim OD-10 exists to prevent. **The comparison is on the question
> answered, never on page count or price alone.**

## 9 · The three prompts

**Written 2026-08-25.** Register matched to
[`product/onboarding-placeholder-prompts.md`](product/onboarding-placeholder-prompts.md) — the locked ICP
library. **These read like the prompts the product tells users to write, because a sample produced from an
unnaturally engineered prompt proves nothing about what a real user gets.**

### How to run them

**Type the prompt. Answer the clarifying questions as written below. Choose the depth at the gate. Nothing
else.**

> **⛔ Do not steer the analysis toward a better answer.** Do not re-prompt, do not coach it, do not run it
> three times and keep the best. **These are the proof layer — if they need help to be good, we need to know
> that, and it is a product finding rather than a marketing problem.**

**The clarifying answers matter more than the prompts.** Each analysis is shadowing a named incumbent, and the
comparison only works if Caspr answers **the question the incumbent does not.** Every "hold" below exists to
stop the analysis drifting back toward the incumbent's own, broader question.

---

### 9.1 · Investors · Study · $80

> **AI clinical documentation — is the growth forecast credible, and who captures the value? I'm screening the sector.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Depth at the gate** | **Study** |
| **Shadows** | Mordor Intelligence, *AI In Clinical Documentation (2026–2031)* — **$4,750**, 180pp, April 2026 |
| **The incumbent's answer** | **$0.98bn (2025) → $3.05bn (2031), 21.46% CAGR** |

**Hold these if asked:**
- **Global.** Do not narrow to one country
- **Ambient scribing, CDI and transcription** — the clinical-documentation core, not all of healthcare AI
- **Through 2031**, to sit against the incumbent's own window
- **Vendors and buyers both.** *"Who captures the value"* is the half a market-size report does not answer

**⛔ Must not drift into:** a market-size report. **The incumbent already is one, and a better market-size
report is not the argument.** The question is whether the forecast survives contact with the sources — and a
21.46% CAGR is a claim, not a fact.

> **The line this analysis exists to earn:** *the market it describes is smaller than forty-nine copies of the
> report describing it.* **Only publish that if the analysis supports it.**

### 9.2 · Consultants · Study · $80

> **Category read on premium ready meals in Southeast England — size, growth and who's winning. Sourced.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Depth at the gate** | **Study** |
| **Shadows** | Mordor Intelligence, *Europe Ready Meals* — **$4,750**, 110pp, August 2026 |
| **The incumbent's coverage** | **Segments across ten European countries.** It does not go below national level |

**Hold these if asked:**
- **⚠ Southeast England. Not the UK, not Europe.** If Caspr offers to broaden the geography, **decline**
- **Premium only** — not the mass tier
- **Retail ready meals**, not foodservice or meal kits
- **Named players with share where it is available**, estimates labelled as estimates

**⛔ Must not drift into:** a UK or European category read. **The sub-national, sub-category intersection *is*
the demonstration.** The moment it broadens, it becomes the report that already costs $4,750 and the whole
comparison collapses.

> **This one is asked for by name in the research** — `icp-personas.md:536`: *"Syndicated data doesn't always
> cover my specific subcategory… premium ready meals in Southeast England… The analyst fills the gap manually
> every time."* **We are running the buyer's actual sentence.**

**Expect a contradiction, and expect it to be the best part.** The UK ready-meals figures observed live were
**$5.86bn at 4.95% CAGR** against **$6.46bn at 12.4% CAGR** — same market, same base year. **If Caspr surfaces
both rather than averaging them, that page sells the product on its own.**

### 9.3 · The hard case · Brief · $15 — run this one first

> **How big is the US public EV charging market, and how fast is it actually growing?**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Depth at the gate** | **Brief** |
| **Shadows** | IBISWorld, *Electric Vehicle Charging Stations in the US* — **published July 2024** |
| **The incumbent's answer, stated publicly** | **$414.3M**, CAGR **9.9%**, **+12.1% in 2024** |

**Hold these if asked:**
- **United States**
- **Public charging** — not home or private depot
- **Current size and growth rate.** A Brief is one tight question; do not let it become a landscape

**Why this subject, and it is not the obvious one.** **IBISWorld sizes this industry at $414.3 million.
Most publishers size "the US EV charging market" in the billions** — because they are counting different
things: establishment revenue against hardware shipments against energy sold. **That is a definitional gap,
not a disagreement**, and almost no page says which it is using.

> **That distinction is the single sharpest thing Caspr can demonstrate** — [`gtm/index-engine.md`](gtm/index-engine.md)
> §2. *"The most-cited figures measure different things and nobody says so"* is unarguable, and it is exactly
> what an analyst is for.

**Two live findings already attached to this one:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | **The incumbent was published July 2024 and is still on sale** — over two years old, with a forecast window that has partly elapsed. **State the date, never an adjective** |
| **2** | **⚠ Confirm the SKU before publishing any price comparison.** IBISWorld sells a **Spotlight at $800** and a **full Industry Research Report at $2,850.** Which one covers this industry decides whether the fold reads *$15 against $800* or *$15 against $2,850*. **The $800 version is the harder and more honest comparison** — if it is the $2,850, say so and note that no cheaper option exists |

**⛔ Why this runs first.** **A $15 Brief against a 10–20 page $800 Spotlight is the closest thing to
scope-equivalent in the entire market, and it is the only one of the three that could go against us.** If it
does, we need that before a fold ships. **A comparison programme that runs only its flattering cases is what
this whole exercise was written to prevent.**
